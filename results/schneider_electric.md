Despite this, the stock continued to slide this week (from ~257.75 EUR on 16.10. to 244.10 EUR now), reflecting lingering market concerns over the ~22.6bn EUR all-cash PTC acquisition (announced 05.10.2026), its ~5-6bn EUR dilutive capital increase, ~16-17bn EUR added debt, and a flagged Q4 risk from Middle East-related supply chain/inflation pressure. Given the unrealized loss has widened to -7.37% despite strong fundamentals, this looks like sentiment/M&A-integration risk rather than a deterioration of the underlying business; "Halten" (hold) continues to appear more appropriate than reducing the position, while an opportunistic small top-up could be considered by investors comfortable with near-term EPS dilution and integration risk.

## Aktuelle Meldungen
- Schneider Electric released its Q3 2026 trading update on 16.10.2026: record quarterly revenue of €11.46bn, up 16.9% organically, exceeding analyst consensus (avg. €11.14bn).
- This marks the second raise of full-year 2026 targets in 2026 (after the July revision), now guiding to +18% to +21% organic adjusted EBITA growth, +12% to +14% organic revenue growth, and +100 to +120bps margin expansion.
- Energy Management grew +19% organically in Q3; Industrial Automation grew +8%. Regionally, China & East Asia rose +26% organically and North America +20%.
- CEO Olivier Blum highlighted that data-center demand grew at a "triple-digit rate" in Q3, strengthening visibility into 2027/2028 ("une visibilité renforcée pour 2027 et 2028").
- S&P Global Ratings and Moody's confirmed Schneider's credit ratings in early October 2026 with a stable outlook, despite the PTC-related debt increase.
- Management flagged that Middle East disruptions could weigh on Q4 2026 results via supply-chain tension and higher input-cost inflation.
- The definitive agreement to acquire US software firm PTC was signed on 05.10.2026: $205 per share in cash (a ~42.3% premium), implying an equity value of roughly €22.6bn (~€23.7bn including net debt) — Schneider's largest acquisition to date. The stock fell nearly 10% on the announcement day.
- Sources differ on the post-2026 buyback plan: some report a pause in 2027–2028 to help fund PTC, others cite a maintained €2.5–3.5bn buyback envelope through 2030 with €600m planned for 2026 — this detail should be confirmed against the company's own investor communication.

## Management
- CEO Olivier Blum (in post since November 2024, leading the company from Dubai) continues to frame strategy around the convergence of energy and AI, saying electrification needs to accelerate "pour rendre l'énergie plus intelligente" (to make energy more intelligent) in response to IEA projections of sharply rising global electricity demand by 2035.
- Blum personally championed the Q3 results and reiterated that data-center-driven demand underpins confidence for 2027 and 2028, directly linking the PTC and Cognite/AVEVA deals to an industrial-software/AI platform strategy.
- Management reaffirmed its commitment to retaining an A-category credit rating even as leverage rises to fund the PTC transaction, a point reinforced by the early-October rating confirmations from S&P and Moody's.

## Finanzielles
- Q3 2026: Revenue €11.46bn, +16.9% organic growth (record quarter, beat consensus).
- FY2026 guidance (raised 16.10.2026, second raise this year): organic adjusted EBITA growth +18% to +21%; organic revenue growth +12% to +14%; adjusted EBITA margin expansion +100 to +120bps.
- H1 2026 (for context): revenue €21.2bn, organic +14%; Q2 2026 record revenue €11.5bn, organic +17%.
- Segment growth in Q3: Energy Management +19% organic; Industrial Automation +8% organic.
- Regional growth in Q3: China & East Asia +26% organic; North America +20% organic.
- Share price: closed at €264.40 on 15.10.2026 (pre-results), down ~9.2% over the preceding month on PTC-deal concerns; as of 20.10.2026 the price has fallen further to 244.10 EUR.
- PTC acquisition financing: ~€5–6bn new equity (accelerated bookbuilding) plus ~€16–17bn new debt; credit ratings (S&P, Moody's) reaffirmed with stable outlook in early October 2026.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- Schneider is accelerating a push into industrial software/AI, combining the pending PTC acquisition (~€22.6bn, largest ever) with the already-closed Cognite deal ($3.1bn, closed mid-2026) to build out an AVEVA-centered industrial-AI platform.
- Data-center electrification remains the single biggest structural growth driver, with triple-digit demand growth cited for Q3 2026 and management pointing to strengthened visibility into 2027–2028.
- The raised FY2026 guidance (second upgrade this year) signals continued confidence in underlying demand, even as the company absorbs higher leverage and a dilutive capital increase to fund PTC.
- Key risks flagged by the company and press: integration risk from back-to-back large acquisitions (PTC, Cognite/AVEVA), equity dilution from the capital raise, maintaining the A-category credit rating amid rising debt, and potential Q4 2026 headwinds from Middle East-related supply-chain disruption and inflation.
- Market reaction has been more cautious than the operating numbers would suggest, with the share price falling further after the Q3 release and PTC announcement — a gap between strong fundamentals and near-term sentiment that current shareholders should monitor through the PTC closing (expected Q3 2027) and the planned capital increase.

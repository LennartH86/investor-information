## Recent News
- On 16.10.2026 Schneider Electric published its Q3 2026 trading update: record quarterly revenue of €11.46bn, up 16.9% organically (17.9% as reported), beating the company-compiled analyst consensus of roughly €11.13bn.
- This was the second raise of full-year 2026 guidance in 2026 (after a July 30 revision): organic adjusted EBITA growth now targeted at +18% to +21% (previously +14% to +19%), organic revenue growth of +12% to +14%.
- Energy Management grew +18.8-19% organically in Q3; Industrial Automation grew +8.1%. By region, China & East Asia rose +26% organically and North America +20%.
- CEO Olivier Blum said data-center demand grew at a "triple-digit rate" during the quarter, reinforcing visibility into 2027 and 2028.
- S&P Global Ratings and Moody's confirmed Schneider's credit ratings on 06.10.2026 with a stable outlook, despite the PTC-related increase in debt.
- Management flagged that Middle East disruptions could weigh on Q4 2026 results via supply-chain strain and higher input-cost inflation.
- The stock fell nearly 10% on 05.10.2026 when the PTC acquisition was announced, and slipped a further ~2-3% on the day of the Q3 results release despite the guidance raise, continuing a multi-week decline.
- A €600m share buyback tranche had been executed to the amount of ~€497m as of 12.10.2026.

## Management
- Olivier Blum has been CEO since November 2024, after the board removed his predecessor, Peter Herweck, citing disagreements over strategy execution; Herweck had held the role for about 18 months. (Note: the research brief referenced Herweck as CEO — this is outdated; Blum is the current CEO as of this update.)
- Blum frames strategy around the convergence of energy and AI/software, arguing electrification must accelerate to meet rising global electricity demand, and directly links the PTC and Cognite/AVEVA deals to building an industrial-AI/software platform.
- Management reaffirmed its commitment to retaining an A-category credit rating even as leverage rises to fund the PTC transaction, reinforced by the early-October rating confirmations from S&P and Moody's.
- Management also acknowledged Middle East-related geopolitical risk as a watch item for Q4 2026 execution.

## Financials
- Q3 2026: revenue €11.46bn, +16.9% organic growth (record quarter, beat consensus of ~€11.13-11.14bn by ~3%).
- Nine-month 2026 revenue: €32,686m, up 15.0% organically and 12.5% as reported.
- FY2026 guidance (raised 16.10.2026, second raise of the year): organic adjusted EBITA growth +18% to +21%; organic revenue growth +12% to +14%; adjusted EBITA margin expansion +100 to +120bps.
- Segment revenue in Q3: Energy Management €9,615m (+18.8% organic); Industrial Automation €1,845m (+8.1% organic).
- PTC acquisition financing: ~€5-6bn in new equity plus ~€16-17bn in new debt; S&P and Moody's reaffirmed ratings with a stable outlook in early October 2026.
- Share price trajectory: ~€264 pre-results (15.10.2026) → ~€249-258 around the Q3 release (16.10.2026) → current portfolio price of 236.40 EUR, a continued decline of roughly 10% from pre-results levels.

## Strategy & Outlook
- Schneider is accelerating its push into industrial software/AI, combining the pending ~$22.6bn/~€22.6bn PTC acquisition (announced 05.10.2026, the company's largest ever) with the already-closed ~$3.1bn Cognite deal (announced June 2026) to build an AVEVA-centered industrial-AI platform.
- Data-center electrification remains the single biggest structural growth driver, with triple-digit demand growth cited for Q3 2026 and management pointing to strengthened visibility into 2027-2028.
- The PTC deal is expected to close by Q3 2027, subject to PTC shareholder approval and regulatory clearances; sources differ on whether share buybacks will pause in 2027-2028 to help fund the deal or whether the €2.5-3.5bn buyback envelope through 2030 continues — this should be confirmed against the company's own investor communication.
- Key risks flagged by the company and press: integration risk from back-to-back large acquisitions (PTC, Cognite, AVEVA), equity dilution from the planned capital increase, maintaining the A-category credit rating amid rising debt, and Middle East-related supply-chain/inflation headwinds for Q4 2026.
- Market reaction remains more cautious than the operating numbers would suggest — the share price has fallen further since both the PTC announcement and the Q3 release — a gap between strong fundamentals and near-term sentiment that current shareholders should monitor through the PTC closing and capital increase.

## Management
- CEO Olivier Blum (in post since November 2024) continues to position Schneider as an "energy technology" company, emphasizing the shift from pure equipment/infrastructure toward connecting AI tools with the company's legacy hardware and software (Semafor interview, 27.03.2026).
- Blum has cited the need for roughly 10,000 additional terawatts of global energy by 2035 (per IEA estimates) and argues this demand must increasingly be met electrically — underpinning Schneider's strategic rationale for its data-center and grid-modernization push.
- In earlier interviews (Fortune, Oct. 2025) Blum stressed a "speed over perfection" leadership philosophy and credited his background as Chief HR Officer for shaping his management style; no materially new CEO interview specific to November 2026 was found.

## Strategy & Outlook
- Major M&A activity in 2026 is reshaping the portfolio toward industrial software and AI:
  - **Cognite** (agreement 30.06.2026): all-cash acquisition valued at $3.1 billion, to be integrated with AVEVA within the Industrial Automation business.
  - **PTC** (announced October 2026): agreement to acquire the Boston-based software firm for approximately $22.6 billion in equity value (reported >€20 billion) — Schneider's largest acquisition to date, significantly expanding its industrial software/PLM capabilities.
  - **AIdash** (31.07.2026): acquisition of the climate-adaptation software firm for about $350 million.
  - **Uplight / Octopus Energy:** Schneider and Octopus Energy agreed to jointly acquire a majority stake in Uplight; EU approval for joint control was granted around 19.05.2026.
- Strategic focus remains on electrification, data-center/AI infrastructure, grid modernization, and industrial AI/software, aligned with Schneider's "Sustainability Impact" program transitioning into its next phase after completing the 2021–2025 SSI program.
- Continued capital discipline signaled via ongoing weekly share buybacks alongside large-scale, debt- and equity-funded acquisitions, suggesting management is pursuing an aggressive (if well-funded) growth strategy that carries integration and execution risk given the scale of the PTC and Cognite deals.

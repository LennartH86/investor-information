## Aktuelle Meldungen
- LVMH shares fell around 2.5% this week, pushing the group's market capitalization down to roughly 201 billion EUR (about 232 billion USD) and just below L'Oréal, knocking LVMH out of Europe's top 10 listed companies for the first time since 2017 — a symbolic reversal after the group led the region's post-pandemic market boom.
- The share price is now trading around 410 EUR, still close to the six-year low of 405.70 EUR reached on 10.09.2026, with only a modest rebound since then.
- LVMH disclosed treasury share transactions carried out between 7 and 11 September 2026 to the French market regulator (AMF) on 15.09.2026, a routine regulatory filing rather than a strategic move.
- The persistent weakness continues to be attributed to soft Chinese demand: Louis Vuitton sales in China reportedly fell around 30% in July 2026 and remained down roughly 20–25% in August 2026, keeping analyst sentiment cautious.

## Management
- Bernard Arnault (77) remains Chairman and CEO; he has previously indicated he will focus more intensively on succession planning once he reaches around 85, with no new developments on this topic in the current period.
- All five of Bernard Arnault's adult children continue to hold senior roles across the group's various maisons; no new management appointments were reported this week.
- Governance and succession questions (highlighted earlier by Forbes) remain a background topic in investor and media discussion but without fresh statements from Arnault during this reporting period.

## Finanzielles
- No new quarterly figures were published in this period; the next scheduled results (Q3 / nine-month 2026 revenue) are due in October 2026.
- H1 2026 remains the latest reported data: revenue of 38.64 billion EUR (organic +2%), operating margin around 22.5%, and net profit of 5.70 billion EUR, above the consensus estimate of 5.22 billion EUR.
- The stock's continued slide pushed LVMH's market capitalization to about 201 billion EUR this week, below L'Oréal and out of Europe's top 10 listed companies — underscoring how far valuation multiples have compressed versus the group's 2023 peak.
- Earlier in 2026, LVMH increased its stake in Loro Piana to 94% (around 1 billion EUR call option) and agreed to sell the Marc Jacobs brand to WHP Global for approximately 500 million USD, both illustrating active portfolio management alongside the ongoing China-driven headwinds.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- Core strategy remains unchanged: high-quality, desirable products for a global clientele, expansion of "destination stores," and continued cost discipline.
- The group continues to diversify into luxury hospitality via its investment arm L Catterton, including the acquisition of Portugal's Penha Longa Resort and a stake in the French boutique hotel operator Les Domaines de Fontenille, alongside brand-focused moves such as the Loro Piana stake increase and the Marc Jacobs divestiture.
- Market sentiment remains highly sensitive to Chinese demand signals; a sustained stabilization of Louis Vuitton's China sales is still seen as the key trigger for a durable bottom in the share price in the second half of 2026.
- The loss of LVMH's top-10 European market-cap ranking is being read by observers as a symbolic marker of the broader luxury sector slowdown rather than a company-specific event, keeping near-term sentiment fragile ahead of the October trading update.

- **Bewertung:** The stock touched a fresh six-year low of EUR 405.70 on 10 September before rebounding to close at EUR 415.30 the next day (+2.16%), leaving the current price essentially flat over the week and still down roughly 31.6% versus the position's average entry price. Precise, up-to-date P/E and dividend-yield figures were not confirmed this week, but the underlying business remains healthy: H1 2026 revenue of EUR 38.6bn, an operating margin of 22.5% and a stable group net profit of EUR 5.7bn, even as Louis Vuitton's China sales have fallen roughly 20-30% in recent months. Given the already substantial drawdown, a depressed valuation, and no signs of a structural collapse in the brand's earnings power, **Halten** remains the reasonable stance; a selective **Aufstocken** could be considered only once Chinese demand shows clearer signs of stabilization, while an outright reduction seems premature at these already-compressed levels.

## Aktuelle Meldungen
- LVMH shares hit a fresh six-year low of EUR 405.70 on 10 September 2026, before rebounding 2.16% to close at EUR 415.30 on 11 September as investors digested continued weak China growth alongside broader Asia-Pacific softness.
- Analysts continued to cut price targets on the stock following the sell-off, even as shares partially steadied into the week.
- LVMH announced the winners of the 13th edition of the LVMH Prize for Young Fashion Designers 2026: Julie Kegels (LVMH Prize), Lii (Karl Lagerfeld Prize) and Yoshita 1967 (Savoir-faire Prize).
- Maison Fred celebrated its 90th anniversary and Acqua di Parma unveiled its "La Caletta" Summer 2026 collection, part of the Group's ongoing brand-activation calendar.
- On 1 September 2026, LVMH filed its routine monthly disclosure of share transactions (own-share buybacks for the period 24-28 August 2026) with the AMF.

## Management
- Bernard Arnault (77) remains Chairman and CEO; he has previously indicated he intends to focus concretely on succession once he reaches age 85.
- A Forbes commentary (August 2026) continues to frame the succession question at LVMH as fundamentally a "governance issue, not a succession issue."
- Laura Burdese was appointed CEO of Bvlgari, one of several recent management changes across the Group's Maisons.
- At the Group's earnings press conference earlier in 2026, Arnault warned that "2026 won't be simple" given a challenging and unpredictable macro environment, while reaffirming the strategy of premium, highly desirable products, destination stores, and cost discipline.

## Finanzielles
- H1 2026: revenue of EUR 38.6bn; organic growth accelerated to 3% in Q2 (4% excluding the impact of the Middle East conflict); profit from recurring operations of EUR 8.7bn, an operating margin of 22.5%; group share of net profit stable year-on-year at EUR 5.7bn.
- Q1 2026: revenue of EUR 19.1bn with 1% organic growth amid a difficult geopolitical backdrop.
- Watches & Jewellery was the fastest-growing division in Q2, up 11% organically, while Fashion & Leather Goods grew a modest 1% to EUR 8.90bn.
- Louis Vuitton's China sales reportedly fell around 30% in July 2026 and roughly 20-25% in August 2026, with September expected to show a smaller, ~13% year-on-year decline according to analyst estimates — the key overhang behind the stock's six-year lows.
- The share price round-trip this week (six-year low of EUR 405.70 on 10 September, rebound to EUR 415.30 on 11 September) illustrates continued high volatility around China-demand headlines.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- Strategy remains unchanged: high-quality, highly desirable products, continued rollout of destination-style stores, and cost containment as the near-term levers against a softer Chinese consumer.
- Portfolio management continues actively: LVMH increased its stake in Loro Piana to 94% via a EUR 1bn call option (January 2026) and completed the sale of Marc Jacobs to a joint venture between G-III Apparel Group and WHP Global (agreed May 2026, ~$500m deal for G-III), sharpening the Group's brand portfolio.
- Hospitality/lifestyle expansion via L Catterton continues, including the acquisition of the Penha Longa Resort in Portugal (with Cedar Capital Partners) and a reported ~20% investment in French boutique-hotel group Les Domaines de Fontenille alongside Anaïs Ventures, making LVMH the largest shareholder there.
- LVMH also announced land-restoration commitments and a new initiative in Chad tied to COP17, reflecting continued ESG/sustainability positioning alongside its commercial strategy.
- The market remains highly sensitive to Chinese demand data points; a durable stabilization in China, together with confirmation that Fashion & Leather Goods can sustain positive organic growth, remain the key signals to watch for the remainder of 2026.

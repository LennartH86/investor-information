- **Current price:** 389.70 EUR
- **Market value:** 3,507.30 EUR
- **Unrealized gain/loss:** -1,898.18 EUR (-35.12%)

- **Assessment:** With H1 2026 net profit roughly flat at ~€5.7bn and operating margin still a very high 22.5%, the underlying business remains resilient despite the Middle East-driven organic growth drag, but the stock's ~35% drawdown reflects persistent market concerns about the luxury cycle and China demand. The €5.50 interim dividend (payable 3 December 2026) implies a yield of roughly 1.4% on the interim alone (full-year dividend has historically been higher, e.g. €13 in prior years, ~3.3% yield on current price), and Bernard Arnault's family has reportedly increased its stake above 50%, signaling insider confidence at depressed levels — on balance this looks like a Hold (long-term quality compounder at a cyclically depressed valuation) rather than an Add or Reduce until Q3 2026 results (due mid-October) clarify the growth trajectory.

## Recent News
- LVMH's "Journées Particulières" (Private/Open Days) return for a sixth edition on 16–18 October 2026, opening workshops, estates and boutiques (including Céline's first public atelier) in France and abroad to the public free of charge.
- Q3 2026 revenue is typically published in mid-October; as of this update (08.10.2026) it had not yet been released/indexed.
- Wisdom Kaye received the Virgil Abloh Award presented by LVMH at Harlem's Fashion Row's 19th Annual Fashion Show & Style Awards in New York (8 September 2026).
- Laura Burdese was named CEO of Bulgari, effective 1 July 2026 (leadership change, not a new development in October but still shaping the Watches & Jewellery division's momentum).

## Management
- Bernard Arnault (Chairman & CEO) reiterated at the April 2026 AGM that the 2026 outlook is "quite unpredictable" given the Middle East crisis, but expects LVMH to gain market share as in past downturns.
- In a June 2026 podcast interview (LEGEND), Arnault reflected on the 1984 Boussac acquisition that founded the group, noting LVMH has since paid ~€40bn in French taxes and social charges and now employs 220,000 people globally.
- Arnault has publicly criticized French corporate tax policy (surtax on large profits), calling it detrimental to employment and competitiveness.
- On succession, Arnault told CNBC (Dec 2025) he has no immediate handover plans ("See you in ten years"), with roles among his five children to be earned on competence; 2025 bylaw changes allow him to remain board chair until age 85.
- Reports indicate the Arnault family's LVMH stake has risen above 50% following share purchases, coinciding with the stock's decline.

## Financials
- H1 2026 revenue: €38.6bn (-3% reported, +2% organic); Q2 2026 organic growth accelerated to +3% (+4% excluding Middle East impact), with Q2 revenue of €19.5bn.
- Watches & Jewellery was the standout division in Q2 2026, with +11% organic growth to €5.22bn, partly reflecting Tiffany & Co.'s continued push to become the sector leader.
- H1 2026 profit from recurring operations: €8.7bn (-4%), impacted by unfavorable currency movements; operating margin held at 22.5%.
- H1 2026 net profit: ~€5.7bn, broadly stable year-on-year and ahead of analyst (FactSet) expectations.
- Interim dividend of €5.50/share declared, payable 3 December 2026.
- Q3 2026 figures (usually released in October) were not yet available as of this update.

## Strategy & Outlook
- Management points to accelerating US growth, strong Asia (ex-Japan) momentum, and new flagship stores (Louis Vuitton in Beijing and Seoul) as growth drivers, alongside Jonathan Anderson's debut designs for Christian Dior.
- The Middle East conflict remains cited as a key headwind, having caused double-digit sales declines in the region earlier in 2026; management expects LVMH to gain share through the downturn as in prior crises.
- China and emerging regions (including Africa) remain described as key long-term focus areas for growth.
- Portfolio moves include a reported sale of Marc Jacobs to WHP Global/G-III Apparel (terms unconfirmed, ~$1bn valuation reported) and earlier minority investments via LVMH Luxury Ventures (Molli) and Thélios (Mykita), reflecting continued portfolio optimization and incubation of smaller brands.
- Unverified reports suggest Arnault personally holds a stake in Richemont, with speculation (unconfirmed) about wider ambitions; this should be treated cautiously pending confirmation.

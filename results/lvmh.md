## Management
- Bernard Arnault remains Chairman & CEO. He has continued personal share purchases (around EUR 407m / USD 440m reported) following earlier-year price weakness, consistent with his stated January 2026 pledge to push the Arnault family's equity stake in LVMH above 50% during the year.
- An unconfirmed report (RetailDetail EU) says Arnault has also taken a stake in rival Richemont; undated and not independently confirmed, so it is flagged here as a rumor to monitor rather than a fact.
- At the April 2026 AGM, Arnault reiterated that the succession question would not be revisited for "another 7-8 years"; a bylaw change allows him to remain in post until age 85.
- Bernard Arnault publicly denied reports of a family rift following a Le Monde article (July 2026); all five of his children continue to hold senior operating positions across the group. Frédéric Arnault remains CEO of Loro Piana; Jean Arnault's reported move to head LVMH Watches (succeeding Jean-Christophe Babin) remains unconfirmed.
- The Arnault family holding structure continues to control roughly 49-50% of share capital and about 66% of voting rights.

## Finanzielles
- Q3 2026 revenue was EUR 18.504bn versus EUR 18.280bn a year earlier (organic growth ~+1%, with currency adding roughly +2% and changes in consolidation scope subtracting about -2%).
- Nine-month 2026 revenue reached EUR 57.1bn, organic growth +2% (reported revenue -2%); Watches & Jewelry and Selective Retailing led growth, Fashion & Leather Goods continued to decline organically (reported down roughly 5% in Q3), while Wines & Spirits improved.
- Geographically, Europe, the US and Japan grew in Q3 2026, while China remained the softest market, though at least one report (Yahoo Finance/Reuters) suggests Chinese demand is beginning to recover.
- Reuters-sourced estimates continue to put LVMH's 2026 market-capitalization decline at over USD 167bn, primarily linked to the Chinese demand slowdown earlier in the year.
- Confirmed interim dividend of 5.50 EUR/share, payable 03.12.2026 — falling just after the end of this reporting window.
- No reliable current P/E/P/B figures could be sourced this round; the share price (400.65 EUR) remains roughly 33% below the 594.89 EUR average purchase price despite the week's ~5.6% rebound.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- LVMH's group-wide "AI for ALL" strategy, detailed at the "Business of Tomorrow" event in Paris (03.11.2026, published 06.11.2026), remains the most recent strategic initiative communicated, focused on operational efficiency and customer experience across divisions; market reaction to the AI rollout has been described as cautious ("LVMH stock: AI rollout meets luxury market caution").
- Portfolio streamlining continues: the agreed sale of Marc Jacobs to a WHP Global/G-III joint venture (announced 14.05.2026, USD 850m, each buyer taking an equal USD 425m stake) reportedly closed around September 2026, and LVMH confirmed in October 2026 that it had completed the sale of most of DFS's duty-free businesses (including Hong Kong/Macao operations and Japan/Indonesia partnership interests), consistent with the January 2026 plan to exit that unit.
- Earlier portfolio moves — the completed stake increase in Loro Piana to 94% (around EUR 1bn invested, completed January 2026) and the small Les Editions Croque Futur acquisition (30.12.2025) — remain the latest confirmed M&A activity; no new acquisitions were identified in this research round.
- The divergence between a recovering Watches & Jewelry / Wines & Spirits and a still-soft Fashion & Leather Goods division remains the key item to watch, alongside early, unconfirmed signs of improving Chinese demand.
- The next hard data point is the FY2026/Q4 release, tentatively set for 28.01.2027 (not yet officially confirmed by LVMH).

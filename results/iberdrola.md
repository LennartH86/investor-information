- Executive Chairman Ignacio Sánchez Galán remains the public face of strategy, having spoken at CERAWeek and to ElEconomista (March 2026) and at Davos (January 2026), stressing that Europe remains on track to meet its 2030 decarbonization targets and highlighting the resilience of Iberdrola's regulated business model.

## Financials
- Q1 2026: adjusted net profit +11.4% YoY to €1,865m; guidance raised to >8% adjusted net profit growth for the year, led by the Networks division.
- H1 2026: reported net profit +22% YoY to €4.34bn (~US$4.9bn); adjusted net profit +8% to €3.57bn; adjusted EBITDA +7% to over €8bn; electricity distributed up 6.3% YoY.
- H1 2026 capex rose 25% to €7bn, with over 70% directed to UK, US and Brazil networks; grid investment alone grew 42% to nearly €4.4bn.
- Twelve-month rolling investment (to ~May 2026) reached €14.5bn (per company release, "€14.46bn drives 12% growth in reported net profit"), the majority directed to UK and US networks.
- Adjusted net debt stands at approximately €50.3bn following completion of the Mexico thermal-generation divestment (net capital gain of €953m) and the Neoenergia (Brazil) minority buy-out.
- FY2026 guidance confirmed at >8% adjusted net profit growth; consensus points to full-year net profit near €6.8bn, versus roughly €6.3bn for FY2025.
- Next reporting date: nine-month 2026 results on 21 October 2026.

## Strategy & Outlook
- Iberdrola agreed to acquire an 80% stake in Caruna Networks, Finland's largest electricity distributor, for roughly €2bn equity value (~€5bn enterprise value; adds ~€2.5bn to the regulated asset base, ~21% of the Finnish distribution market). The deal is expected to close in Q1 2027 and to be accretive from year one, with ~7% annual net income growth expected from the asset.
- The Caruna deal is being financed partly with proceeds from the Mexico thermal-generation divestment.
- Iberdrola targets 15.5 GW of new renewable capacity additions between 2025 and 2030, having invested €2.2bn in renewables in H1 2026 alone.
- The company plans roughly US$42.2bn of grid infrastructure investment, with more than 70% directed to UK and US networks, reinforcing the shift from thermal generation toward regulated networks and renewables.
- The Neoenergia (Brazil) buy-out (completed mid-May 2026) gives Iberdrola 100% ownership and long-dated visibility from concessions extending more than 30 years.
- Key upcoming catalyst: nine-month 2026 results on 21 October 2026, which should give further visibility on FY2026 guidance and the Caruna integration timeline.

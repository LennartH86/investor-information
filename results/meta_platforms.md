## Aktuelle Meldungen
- Meta shares jumped on the launch of new AI models: "Muse Spark 1.3" (efficiency-focused) and "Muse Voice Transcribe," a real-time audio transcription model announced September 1, 2026, both cited as catalysts for the week's rally.
- Meta began production of its in-house AI chip "Iris" in September 2026, developed with Broadcom (design) and TSMC (fabrication), targeting a doubling of compute capacity to 14 gigawatts by 2027 and reduced reliance on Nvidia/AMD GPUs; new chip generations are planned roughly every six months through 2027.
- The ~$18 billion settlement with U.S. states over teen social-media harm continues to be viewed positively by analysts (Morgan Stanley) as clearing the way for faster AI product rollouts; Meta is making related changes for teen users following the settlement.
- Continued heavy AI/data-center buildout remains the dominant investor narrative, with ad revenue growth still solid even as AI investment stays elevated.

## Management
- CEO Mark Zuckerberg continues to frame Meta's AI capex around "personal superintelligence" — deeply personalized AI assistants, creative tools, and business agents across Meta's apps.
- In a July 2026 Bloomberg interview, Zuckerberg said exploring an AI cloud/compute-rental business "makes sense" given how lucrative external compute-rental offers currently are.
- At Meta's May 2026 shareholder meeting, Zuckerberg said entering the cloud computing market is "definitely on the table."
- Zuckerberg has repeatedly highlighted smart glasses as a bright spot, noting AI-glasses sales tripled in 2025.

## Finanzielles
- Q1 2026: total revenue $56.31 billion (+33% YoY); operating income $22.87 billion (+30% YoY); average price per ad +12% YoY; DAP 3.56 billion (average, March 2026, +4% YoY).
- Q2 2026: total revenue $60.8 billion (+28% YoY); DAP 3.60 billion (average, June 2026, +3% YoY); ad impressions +14% YoY, average price per ad +12% YoY; total expenses +55% YoY, including $2.4 billion in legal-proceeding charges and $1.2 billion in severance.
- 2026 capital expenditure guidance stands at $125–145 billion (raised from an earlier $115–135 billion range), driven by AI infrastructure buildout including the Iris chip program.
- The $18 billion teen-safety settlement is a known, bounded near-term legal cost that reduces litigation uncertainty going forward.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- Continued heavy investment in AI infrastructure, including the in-house "Iris" chip (with Broadcom/TSMC), now entering production, aimed at reducing dependency on third-party AI hardware.
- New consumer-facing AI model launches (Muse Spark 1.3, Muse Voice Transcribe) signal an accelerating product cadence intended to showcase AI investment payoff to investors.
- Strategic AI-focused M&A remains active but not friction-free: Meta's ~$2 billion Manus acquisition has faced complications after Chinese regulators ordered the deal unwound; the ~$15 billion Scale AI stake remains a core pillar of Meta's AI agent strategy under Alexandr Wang.
- Meta continues weighing entry into an AI cloud/compute-rental business as a potential new revenue stream beyond advertising.
- Central open question for investors remains when substantial AI capex will translate into measurable returns, alongside scrutiny of the "personal superintelligence" strategy.

## Aktuelle Meldungen
- Meta and a coalition of U.S. states reached an ~$18 billion settlement over teen social-media harm claims; Meta commits to concrete platform changes for under-18 users, including a two-hour daily usage cap, disabling extreme cosmetic-surgery/makeup filters, and tighter age verification. Morgan Stanley noted the settlement could clear the way for new AI product launches by removing legal uncertainty.
- Meta plans to begin production of its in-house AI chip "Iris" in September 2026, developed with Broadcom (design) and TSMC (fabrication), with new chip generations planned roughly every six months through 2027.
- Meta is deploying closed-loop liquid cooling in its AI data centers to improve energy efficiency for AI workloads.
- Product updates: five new Meta Quest games added to the Meta Horizon+ subscription for fall; WhatsApp rolled out enhanced account security features, including stronger two-step verification and additional context for calls from unknown numbers.
- Analysts remain cautiously optimistic, citing continued ad revenue growth even as heavy AI investment persists.

## Management
- CEO Mark Zuckerberg has continued to frame Meta's massive AI capex around the pursuit of "personal superintelligence" — AI systems intended to function as deeply personalized assistants, creative tools, and business agents across Meta's ecosystem.
- In a July 2026 Bloomberg interview, Zuckerberg said exploring an AI cloud business "makes sense," noting that compute rental offers are currently so lucrative that renting out capacity may in some cases be more valuable than internal use.
- At the January 2026 earnings call, Zuckerberg said 2026 would be the year AI "dramatically" changes how Meta works, with flattened teams and AI tools allowing projects that once required large teams to be completed by a single highly capable person.
- Zuckerberg has highlighted the smart-glasses business as a bright spot, noting AI-glasses sales tripled in 2025 and rank among the fastest-growing consumer electronics categories in history.

## Finanzielles
- Q1 2026: total revenue $56.31 billion (+33% YoY); operating income $22.87 billion (+30% YoY); average price per ad +12% YoY; DAP 3.56 billion (average, March 2026, +4% YoY).
- Q2 2026: total revenue $60.8 billion (+28%, or +27% constant currency); total expenses $42 billion (+55% YoY), including $2.4 billion in legal-proceeding charges and $1.2 billion in severance costs; DAP 3.60 billion (average, June 2026, +3% YoY); ad impressions +14% YoY, average price per ad +12% YoY.
- 2026 capital expenditure guidance was raised to a range of $125–145 billion (up from an earlier $115–135 billion range), driven by AI infrastructure buildout; some reports cite a figure as high as $135–145 billion for the full year.
- The $18 billion teen-safety settlement adds a known, bounded near-term legal cost but reduces litigation uncertainty going forward.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- Continued heavy investment in AI infrastructure, including the in-house "Iris" chip (with Broadcom/TSMC) rolling out from September 2026, aimed at reducing dependency on third-party AI hardware over time.
- Strategic AI-focused M&A remains active, though not without friction: Meta's roughly $2 billion Manus acquisition has faced complications after Chinese regulators ordered the deal unwound, and Meta has been dismantling the arrangement to comply. The ~$15 billion Scale AI stake, with Scale AI founder Alexandr Wang now leading Meta's superintelligence unit, remains a core pillar of Meta's AI agent strategy.
- Meta continues weighing entry into an AI cloud/compute-rental business, potentially opening a new revenue stream beyond advertising given strong external demand for compute capacity.
- The teen-safety settlement is expected to reduce regulatory/legal overhang and may enable faster rollout of new AI-driven consumer products, per Morgan Stanley commentary.
- The central open question for investors remains when the substantial AI capex will translate into measurable returns, alongside continued scrutiny of Meta's "personal superintelligence" strategy under Zuckerberg.

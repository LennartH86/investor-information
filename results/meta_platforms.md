## Aktuelle Meldungen
- Meta officially launched "Muse," an autonomous AI personal agent, on the evening of September 8, 2026. Muse can independently book travel, send emails, complete web forms, and manage payments across iOS, Android, and a dedicated web app.
- Muse introduces Meta's first tiered AI subscription model, with paid plans at $20 and $100 per month alongside a free tier.
- Meta stock surged (up ~3.6% pre-market on the Muse news, and reportedly up ~8.3% over the prior week) partly on the AI launch and continued relief over the $18 billion teen-safety settlement clearing legal overhang.
- Ahead of Muse, Meta released "Muse Spark 1.3" on September 2, 2026, which the company's chief AI officer described as highly competitive with frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI.
- Meta Connect 2026, a two-day livestream event (September 23–24, 2026) showcasing new AI, AI-glasses, and VR breakthroughs, is scheduled for later this month.
- Meta began production of its in-house AI chip "Iris" (co-developed with Broadcom on design and TSMC on fabrication) in September 2026, targeting a doubling of compute capacity to 14 gigawatts by 2027 and reduced reliance on Nvidia/AMD GPUs.

## Management
- CEO Mark Zuckerberg discussed Muse in depth on the Sources podcast (September 8, 2026), describing his ambition to "give every person in the world a very capable personal agent."
- In late July 2026, Zuckerberg ran a broader media campaign defending Meta's AI strategy, telling the New York Times that AI discourse is "overwhelmingly filled with doom," and penning a Wall Street Journal op-ed plus interviews with both outlets.
- In a July 2026 Bloomberg interview, Zuckerberg said exploring an AI cloud/compute-rental business "makes sense" given current market demand for external compute.
- At Meta's May 2026 shareholder meeting, Zuckerberg said entering the cloud computing market is "definitely on the table."

## Finanzielles
- Q1 2026: total revenue $56.31 billion (+33% YoY); operating income $22.87 billion (+30% YoY); ad impressions +19% YoY, average price per ad +12% YoY; DAP 3.56 billion (average, March 2026, +4% YoY).
- Q2 2026: total revenue $60.80 billion (+28% YoY); operating income $18.78 billion (‑8% YoY, driven by higher costs and expenses); ad impressions +14% YoY, average price per ad +12% YoY; DAP 3.60 billion (average, June 2026, +3% YoY).
- 2026 capital expenditure guidance stands at $125–145 billion (raised from an earlier $115–135 billion range), driven by AI infrastructure buildout including the Iris chip program and Meta Superintelligence Labs.
- The $18 billion teen-safety settlement remains a known, bounded near-term legal cost that reduces litigation uncertainty going forward.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- Muse's launch and its new $20/$100 tiered subscription model mark Meta's first direct-to-consumer AI monetization attempt beyond advertising, a key metric for investors to watch going forward.
- Continued heavy investment in AI infrastructure, including the in-house "Iris" chip (with Broadcom/TSMC), now entering production, aimed at reducing dependency on third-party AI hardware.
- Meta Connect 2026 (September 23–24) is the next major catalyst, expected to showcase further AI, AI-glasses, and VR developments.
- Strategic AI-focused M&A remains active but not friction-free: Meta's ~$2 billion Manus acquisition has faced complications after Chinese regulators ordered the deal unwound; the ~$15 billion Scale AI stake remains a core pillar of Meta's AI agent strategy.
- Meta continues weighing entry into an AI cloud/compute-rental business as a potential new revenue stream beyond advertising.
- Central open question for investors remains when substantial AI capex will translate into measurable returns, alongside scrutiny of margin compression (Q2 operating income declined YoY despite revenue growth).

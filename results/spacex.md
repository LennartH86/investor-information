## Management
- Board change: Don Harrison (board member for over a decade) departed the Board effective 4 November 2026; no successor was named in the release.
- Elon Musk remains CEO/CTO and continues to be the primary public face of the company, giving frequent interviews (e.g., Ron Baron conference, prior Economist and Sean Hannity Show appearances) emphasizing AI, Mars, and Starship timelines.
- SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen has given investor-facing interviews discussing the company's post-IPO financial profile.

## Financials
- Q2 2026 (first quarterly report as a public company, published 4 August 2026): revenue ~$7.8 billion (+92% YoY), of which Starlink contributed ~$4.3 billion; net loss of $541 million (narrower than the $1 billion loss a year earlier); Q2 capital expenditure of ~$18.4 billion, including ~$15.8 billion on AI infrastructure.
- Q3 2026: revenue of $14.6 billion, up 161% YoY — a sharp acceleration versus Q2, though the source did not specify a net income/loss figure for the quarter.
- SpaceX's IPO (June 2026) raised over $85 billion and valued the company at roughly $1.75–1.8 trillion; the stock has since reportedly declined about 8% from that level, with one estimate putting post-earnings valuation around $1.53 trillion.
- Reported cash position of roughly $100 billion, though this figure could not be confirmed against a primary filing.
- Heavy capex concentrated on AI infrastructure is a recurring theme and a source of investor caution despite strong top-line growth.

## Strategy & Outlook
- AI expansion: SpaceX acquired xAI in an all-stock deal (February 2026) reported to value the combined company at ~$1.25 trillion, aimed at building space-based AI data centers and tying xAI's growth to SpaceX's cash flow.
- Further AI push: a planned $60 billion all-stock acquisition of AI coding startup Cursor/Anysphere was announced around June 2026; reports disagree on whether this deal has closed.
- Musk has outlined ambitions including a first batch of AI satellites "next year," a Mars landing target in roughly five years, and crewed Moon trips within a decade (unconfirmed secondary reporting).
- Starship remains central to near-term newsflow, with the next test flight tentatively set for mid-to-late November 2026 pending FAA licensing.
- Starlink continues to scale (13,096 satellites launched as of early November 2026) and remains the largest revenue contributor within the business.

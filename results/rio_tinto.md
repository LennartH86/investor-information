## Aktuelle Meldungen
- **China's CMRG (China Mineral Resources Group)** ordered a number of steel mills to suspend annual supply-contract negotiations with Rio Tinto from early September, the latest instance of the state-backed buyer using its leverage over more than half of China's iron ore imports (1.2+ bn tonnes/year) to extract better pricing and terms, including yuan-denominated contracts, from Rio Tinto. The stock fell around 3% on 10 September 2026 as investors weighed this dispute against progress on the Winu project.
- Rio Tinto agreed to **acquire the Aurukun bauxite project in Queensland from Glencore and Mitsubishi Development** (financial terms undisclosed); the project sits ~200 km south of Rio Tinto's existing Weipa operations on Cape York and still requires a mining lease and further permitting/community approvals before construction, but strengthens the bauxite pipeline as Rio Tinto winds down its Gove mine in the Northern Territory by 2030.
- Rio Tinto secured **Traditional Owner consent for the Winu copper-gold project** in Western Australia, alongside a new **Interim Modernised Agreement with the Ngarlawangga Aboriginal Corporation** (signed 8 September 2026) covering the West Angelas mine area — a jointly designed co-management framework updating a 2011 participation agreement with additional long-term financial support for the Ngarlawangga People.
- Rio Tinto marked **60 years of operations in Western Australia** (22 August 2026), commemorating the first iron ore shipment from Dampier to Japan in 1966, and expanded the **Rio Tinto Perth Wildcats Academy** into the Pilbara region.
- The **Bell Bay Aluminium smelter power negotiation** in Tasmania remains unresolved, with the current one-year deal with Hydro Tasmania expiring 31 December 2026 and over 550 direct jobs at risk absent a longer-term agreement.
- The revived **~260 bn USD Glencore merger talks collapsed** in early September after the two miners failed to agree terms Rio Tinto shareholders would benefit from — the third failed attempt at a tie-up (following 2014 and 2024) — even as the two companies proceed separately on the unrelated Aurukun bauxite transaction.

## Management
- **Simon Trott** remains Chief Executive; his cost-discipline programme has banked 870 million USD of efficiencies, targeting a 1.8 billion USD annualised run-rate by year-end 2026, and he has publicly framed Rio Tinto as a "good bet" on AI-driven copper demand.
- Trott led the decision to walk away from the Glencore merger talks while simultaneously pursuing smaller, accretive bolt-on deals such as Aurukun — a contrast management has framed as capital discipline over deal-making for scale.
- **CFO Peter Cunningham** continues alongside Trott in investor communications following the H1 2026 results.
- No new senior leadership changes were disclosed in the sources reviewed this period, following the August appointment of **Trudi Charles** as Chief Legal Officer, Governance & Corporate Affairs.

## Finanzielles
- **H1 2026 underlying EBITDA: 14.8 bn USD, up 28%** year on year, driven by higher copper and aluminium prices plus productivity gains.
- **Free cash flow up 75%** in H1 2026, described by management as a step-change supporting higher shareholder returns.
- **Copper equivalent production +3%** in H1 2026, with Oyu Tolgoi delivering more than 30% growth for the half; Pilbara iron ore production was the highest first half since 2018.
- **870 million USD of productivity benefits already banked**, on track for the 1.8 bn USD annualised run-rate by end of 2026.
- Ongoing capital commitments include roughly **297.8 million USD** pro-rata for the CBA aluminium stake, the undisclosed Aurukun bauxite consideration, and the previously announced 300+ million USD Nemaska Lithium investment in Québec; group capex is guided at up to 10 bn USD across 2025–2026.
- No explicit current P/E, P/B or dividend yield figures were disclosed in the sources reviewed for this period.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- **Full-year 2026 guidance maintained**, with iron ore sales and lithium volumes trending higher, though the CMRG contract stand-off with China adds fresh uncertainty to near-term iron ore pricing and volumes.
- **China relationship now a key swing factor:** CMRG's directive to steel mills to pause negotiations is the latest in a recurring pattern of the Chinese state buyer using its concentrated purchasing power during annual contract talks — an outcome to watch closely given China's importance to Rio Tinto's iron ore revenue.
- **Bauxite/aluminium portfolio reshaping:** the Aurukun acquisition (subject to Queensland regulatory approval) extends the bauxite pipeline as the Gove mine is wound down by 2030, continuing the low-carbon aluminium strategy alongside the CBA joint acquisition with Chalco.
- **Social licence progress:** the Winu Traditional Owner consent and the Ngarlawangga Interim Modernised Agreement reduce permitting/community risk for two significant WA growth assets.
- **Tasmanian aluminium risk:** the unresolved Bell Bay power agreement remains a near-term watch item, with a failure to strike a deal by year-end threatening smelter closure and over 550 jobs.
- **Lithium build-out** continues in Québec (Nemaska) alongside Rincon, Sal de Vida and Fénix 1B in Argentina, targeting 200,000 t/y LCE by 2028.
- Key watch items: **resolution of the CMRG/China iron ore negotiations**, Queensland regulatory approval for Aurukun, the Bell Bay power outcome, and delivery on the 1.8 bn USD productivity run-rate.

## Management
- **Simon Trott** remains Chief Executive (succeeded Jakob Stausholm in 2026); some secondary sources still mistakenly reference Stausholm, but primary company releases confirm Trott as CEO.
- Trott continues to drive **cost discipline**, with USD 870 million in efficiencies banked by mid-2026 toward an annualised USD 1.8 billion year-end run-rate target.
- Under Trott, Rio Tinto is **consolidating from five product groups into three core business units**: iron ore, aluminium & lithium, and copper — intended to sharpen focus on supply capacity, competitiveness and growth.
- At the 2026 AGM, Trott acknowledged safety shortfalls following fatalities at company sites (including at Simandou and Kennecott during Q1 2026) and reiterated the ambition to unlock **more than USD 10 billion** through portfolio optimisation and divestments.

## Financials
- **H1 2026 results (last confirmed):** underlying EBITDA up 28% to USD 14.8 billion; net earnings up 47% to USD 6.7 billion; free cash flow sharply higher (reported variously as USD 3.8–6.9 billion depending on source); interim dividend up 43% to USD 3.4 billion (USD 2.11/share), the highest in four years.

## Strategy & Outlook
- **Portfolio optimisation continues:** the CBA joint acquisition with Chalco, the earlier Arcadium Lithium acquisition, and the agreed Aurukun bauxite purchase (from a Glencore/Mitsubishi Development joint venture, announced September 2026) all expand Rio Tinto's aluminium and energy-transition footprint; management is targeting roughly USD 5–10 billion in asset sales by end-2026.
- **Potential new Pilbara iron ore collaboration with BHP**, reportedly exploring a joint plan to mine up to 200 million tonnes, subject to a final investment decision — a development to monitor closely in coming weeks.
- **Growth capex:** continued capital expenditure guidance of up to USD 11.0 billion in both 2026 and 2027, including the previously approved USD 180 million Norman Creek access project at Amrun (first production targeted 2027) and a feasibility study on the Kangwinan project (up to 20 Mtpa of bauxite capacity from 2029 at the earliest).
- **Iron ore capacity expansion:** mid-term guidance targets 425–440 million tonnes of sales capacity (up from 346 million tonnes in 2025), anchored by Simandou (Guinea) and Rhodes Ridge (Western Australia).
- **M&A stance:** a Glencore merger revival looks unlikely for now; management signals that further M&A could be needed to secure long-term copper growth beyond 2030.
- **Cost discipline remains central**, with the group progressing toward its USD 1.8 billion year-end productivity target and the broader ambition to unlock over USD 10 billion from the asset base.
- Key near-term watch item: **Q3 2026 production results on 13 October 2026**.

- Rio Tinto is seen as **unlikely to revive merger talks with Glencore** now that their standstill agreement has expired; management remains focused on unlocking more than USD 10 billion through portfolio optimization and organic copper growth ([news.metal.com](https://news.metal.com/en/newscontent/104044548-rio-tinto-unlikely-to-revive-glencore-merger-talks-as-standstill-expires)).
- The joint acquisition with Chalco of Votorantim's controlling stake (~68.6%) in Companhia Brasileira de Alumínio (CBA, total ~USD 902.6 million, Rio Tinto's share ~USD 297.8 million) continues to be referenced as part of Rio Tinto's low-carbon aluminium expansion in Brazil.

## Management
- **Simon Trott** remains Chief Executive (succeeded Jakob Stausholm in 2025/2026); some secondary sources still mistakenly reference Stausholm, but primary company releases confirm Trott as CEO.
- Trott continues to drive **cost discipline**, with the group progressing toward an annualised USD 1.8 billion year-end productivity target.
- Under Trott, Rio Tinto has **restructured from five product groups into three core business units**: iron ore, aluminium, and energy transition minerals (lithium and copper) — intended to sharpen focus on supply capacity, competitiveness and growth.
- At the 2026 AGM in Perth, Trott addressed three fatalities at company sites since the prior meeting (including at Simandou in Guinea and Kennecott in Utah), stating the deaths show the company is "not where it needs to be" on safety, and announced a renewed focus on critical safety controls.

## Finanzielles
- **H1 2026 results (last confirmed):** underlying EBITDA up 28% to USD 14.8 billion; net earnings up 47% to USD 6.7 billion; free cash flow sharply higher (reported variously as USD 3.8–6.9 billion depending on source); interim dividend up 43% to USD 3.4 billion, the highest in four years.
- **Q2 2026 production:** global iron ore sales up 5% year-on-year to 89 million tonnes; copper production down ~7% to 213,000 tonnes, with Escondida (Chile) down 13% on lower ore grades.
- **Q1 2026 production:** iron ore output up 13% to nearly 79 million tonnes (two tropical cyclones slowed shipments); copper equivalent production up 9% year-on-year, mainly from the continuing ramp-up of the Oyu Tolgoi underground copper mine in Mongolia; full-year 2026 guidance across all commodities left unchanged.
- No explicit current P/E or P/B ratio was disclosed in the sources reviewed this period; **Q3 2026 production figures (13 October 2026)** will be the next concrete data point.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- **Portfolio optimisation continues:** the CBA joint acquisition with Chalco, the earlier Arcadium Lithium acquisition, and the agreed Aurukun bauxite purchase (announced September 2026) all expand Rio Tinto's aluminium and energy-transition footprint; management is targeting roughly USD 5–10 billion in asset sales as it pursues an annual copper output target of 1 million tonnes by 2030.
- **Potential new Pilbara iron ore collaboration with BHP**, exploring a joint plan to mine up to 200 million tonnes, subject to a final investment decision — a development to monitor closely in coming weeks.
- **Growth capex:** capital expenditure guidance remains up to USD 11.0 billion in both 2026 and 2027.
- **M&A stance:** a Glencore merger revival looks unlikely for now; management signals that further M&A could be needed to secure long-term copper growth beyond 2030.
- **Cost discipline remains central**, with the group progressing toward its USD 1.8 billion year-end productivity target and the broader ambition to unlock over USD 10 billion from the asset base.
- Key near-term watch item: **Q3 2026 production results on 13 October 2026**.

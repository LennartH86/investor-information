## Aktuelle Meldungen / Recent News
- Q3 2026 production report (14 October 2026): Pilbara iron ore sales reached 85.7 million tonnes, up 2% year-on-year and above the Visible Alpha consensus estimate of 84.41 Mt; full-year 2026 Pilbara sales guidance of 323–338 Mt was left unchanged.
- Oyu Tolgoi copper production rose 29% year-to-date, running at roughly a 450 kt annualised rate in the quarter; the Simandou iron ore project in Guinea reached an annualised run-rate of about 10 Mtpa (100% basis).
- Copper output figures for Q3 are disputed between sources: one report cites roughly flat output at ~205,000 tonnes (growth at Oyu Tolgoi offset by weaker output at Escondida and Kennecott), another cites a 7% year-on-year decline to ~213,000 tonnes — treat with caution pending the company's own detailed release.
- Rio Tinto agreed (announced around 9 September 2026) to acquire the Aurukun bauxite project in Queensland from a Glencore–Mitsubishi Development joint venture; financial terms were undisclosed and the deal remains subject to regulatory approval.
- Rio Tinto and Chalco agreed to jointly acquire Votorantim's controlling stake in Brazilian aluminium producer CBA, in a deal reported at around USD 902.6 million in total, with Rio's share at roughly USD 297.8 million.
- Rio Tinto is reported to be unlikely to revive merger talks with Glencore in the near term as a standstill period expires; the company's stated priority is unlocking more than USD 10 billion via portfolio optimisation and divestments alongside organic copper growth.

## Management
- Simon Trott has taken over as CEO (succession announced in 2025, effective since mid-2025), succeeding Jakob Stausholm, who led the company from 2021. Sources vary on the exact handover date; some recent H1 2026 commentary still referenced Stausholm, indicating the transition period extended into 2026.
- Trott visited Mongolia shortly after taking the role, underscoring the strategic importance of the Oyu Tolgoi copper project.
- At the AGM in Perth on 6 May 2026, Trott addressed safety, acknowledging three fatalities at company sites since the prior annual meeting and announcing a renewed focus on critical controls for safety risks across operations; two worker deaths were also reported during Q1 2026 (at Simandou and Kennecott).
- The company has reorganised its operations into three product groups — iron ore, aluminium & lithium, and copper — each now led by its own dedicated chief executive, reflecting a push toward sharper operational accountability and the growing strategic weight of copper and lithium within the portfolio.

## Finanzielles / Financials
- H1 2026 (reported 29 July 2026): underlying EBITDA up 28% year-on-year to USD 14.8bn; net earnings up 47% to USD 6.7bn.
- Interim dividend of USD 3.4bn declared for H1 2026 — the highest interim payout in four years, signalling management confidence in cash generation.
- H1 2026 Pilbara iron ore output reported between 162.3 Mt and 169.9 Mt depending on source (roughly a 5% increase versus H1 2025); copper-equivalent production rose about 3% for the half, though Q2 copper alone fell roughly 7% year-on-year due to maintenance and mine sequencing.
- Capital optimisation programme: more than USD 2bn released so far in 2026 against a stated USD 5–10bn target range, with management guiding to roughly USD 5bn released by year-end 2026; analysts note this full target still depends on finalising binding agreements for further divestments.
- Productivity programme remains on track for a USD 1.8bn annualised benefit run-rate by the end of 2026.
- Q4 2025 results (reported January 2026) had already shown a record quarterly Pilbara output of 89.7 Mt and copper output of 240 kt, beating estimates by roughly 11%, setting up the strong momentum carried into 2026.

## Strategie & Ausblick / Strategy & Outlook
- Continued ramp-up of growth assets: Oyu Tolgoi (Mongolia, copper) and Simandou (Guinea, iron ore) are central to medium-term production growth, with both now contributing meaningfully to volumes in 2026.
- Portfolio optimisation and divestment programme targeting USD 5–10bn in capital release by year-end 2026, intended to fund growth capex and shareholder returns rather than large-scale M&A.
- Bolt-on acquisitions continue selectively in bauxite/aluminium (Aurukun, CBA stake) to strengthen the downstream aluminium value chain, while a renewed bid for Glencore is not expected near-term; management's stated priority is organic copper growth over large mergers.
- Full-year 2026 production guidance across commodities was reaffirmed as of the Q3 update, suggesting management sees limited near-term operational risk to the existing plan.
- Safety remains an explicit strategic priority following multiple fatalities in 2026, with a renewed focus on critical risk controls across sites — an area investors should continue to monitor given its link to both operational and reputational risk.

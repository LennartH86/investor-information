## Aktuelle Meldungen
- Rio Tinto and Glencore **abandoned their revived ~260 bn USD merger talks** in early September 2026 after failing to agree terms that would deliver value to Rio Tinto shareholders; Glencore said the proposed terms (under which Rio Tinto would keep the chairman and CEO roles) "significantly undervalued" its relative contribution. This marks the third failed attempt at a tie-up between the two miners (following 2014 and 2024).
- Rio Tinto shares gained modestly in early September as **Singapore iron ore futures rose to ~98.75 USD**, with ASX-listed shares up ~0.96% to AUD 176.56 and NYSE shares trading around USD 102.75 on 3 September 2026.
- The **Bell Bay Aluminium smelter power negotiation** in Tasmania remains unresolved and is drawing growing local and political attention (over 550 direct jobs at risk); the current one-year deal with state-owned Hydro Tasmania expires 31 December 2026, and Rio Tinto's offer reportedly sits about 60 million USD below Hydro Tasmania's ask despite more than two years of talks on a longer-term 10-year agreement.
- Rio Tinto marked **60 years of operations in Western Australia** (22 August 2026), a milestone highlighted alongside continued record Pilbara iron ore output.
- The **CBA joint acquisition with Chalco** (Votorantim's 68.596% stake in Companhia Brasileira de Alumínio, Rio Tinto's pro-rata share ~297.8 million USD) continues to progress as part of the low-carbon aluminium strategy.

## Management
- **Simon Trott** remains Chief Executive; his cost-discipline programme has banked 870 million USD of efficiencies, targeting a 1.8 billion USD annualised run-rate by year-end 2026.
- Trott led the decision to walk away from the Glencore merger talks, citing an inability to reach terms that would create shareholder value — the second time under his tenure that a major combination has been shelved.
- **CFO Peter Cunningham** continues alongside Trott in investor communications following the H1 2026 results.
- **Trudi Charles** joined as Chief Legal Officer, Governance & Corporate Affairs (effective 1 August 2026), succeeding Isabelle Deschamps, as part of the reshaped executive team under Trott.

## Finanzielles
- **H1 2026 underlying EBITDA: 14.8 bn USD, up 28%** year on year, driven by higher copper and aluminium prices plus productivity gains.
- **Free cash flow up 75%** in H1 2026, described by management as a step-change supporting higher shareholder returns.
- **Copper equivalent production +3%** in H1 2026, with Oyu Tolgoi delivering more than 30% growth for the half.
- **Highest first-half Pilbara iron ore production since 2018**, aided by the productivity improvement programme.
- **870 million USD of productivity benefits already banked**, on track for the 1.8 bn USD annualised run-rate by end of 2026.
- Ongoing capital commitments include roughly **297.8 million USD** pro-rata for the CBA stake (aluminium) and the previously announced 300+ million USD Nemaska Lithium investment in Québec; group capex is guided at up to 10 bn USD across 2025–2026.
- No explicit current P/E, P/B or dividend yield figures were disclosed in the sources reviewed for this period.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- **Full-year 2026 guidance maintained**, with iron ore sales and lithium volumes trending higher.
- **M&A discipline reaffirmed:** walking away from the Glencore mega-merger signals management's stated preference for shareholder-value-accretive deals over scale for its own sake; bolt-on moves (CBA, Nemaska) remain the preferred growth vehicle instead.
- **Tasmanian aluminium risk:** an unresolved Bell Bay power agreement is a near-term watch item, with a failure to strike a deal by year-end threatening smelter closure and over 550 jobs.
- **Lithium build-out:** continued investment in Québec (Nemaska Lithium) alongside Rincon, Sal de Vida and Fénix 1B in Argentina, targeting 200,000 t/y LCE by 2028, building on the 6.7 bn USD Arcadium Lithium acquisition completed in October 2024.
- Continued focus on **cost reduction, productivity and non-core asset disposals** to fund growth while protecting shareholder returns.
- Key watch items: **outcome of the Bell Bay power negotiations**, any renewed Glencore-related speculation, Chinese iron ore demand, integration progress on the CBA and Nemaska investments, and delivery on the 1.8 bn USD productivity run-rate.

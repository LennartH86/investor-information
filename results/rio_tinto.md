## Aktuelle Meldungen / Recent News
- Q3 2026 production report (released 14 October 2026): Pilbara iron ore sales of 85.7 Mt, up 2% y/y and above the Visible Alpha consensus of 84.41 Mt; 2026 Pilbara sales guidance of 323–338 Mt reaffirmed. One source cites global iron ore sales of 90.8 Mt (+3% y/y) and Pilbara production (as opposed to sales) of 83.4 Mt — figures differ by source/measure, so treat exact tonnages with some caution.
- Copper output for Q3 is reported inconsistently across sources: roughly flat at ~205,000 t (one Reuters-based account) versus a ~7% y/y decline to ~213,000 t (another outlet); Oyu Tolgoi copper output rose 27–29% in the quarter/year-to-date, offsetting weaker output at Escondida and Kennecott. 2026 copper guidance of 800–870 kt was maintained.
- Simandou iron ore project (Guinea) reached an annualised run-rate of about 10 Mtpa (100% basis) during the quarter; the company still targets 60 Mtpa in H2 2028.
- Alumina output rose ~10% y/y; bauxite output fell ~4% y/y to 15.7 Mt (full-year guidance of 58–61 Mt unchanged); lithium carbonate equivalent output rose 33% year-to-date to 40,300 t despite weather disruption in Q3.
- Capital optimisation programme: more than USD 2bn released year-to-date against a USD 5–10bn target, with management still guiding to roughly USD 5bn (about A$7bn) released by year-end 2026.
- Shares traded roughly flat around the production release (e.g. ~A$160.67, -0.2%, in early trading on the announcement day); NYSE-listed shares closed near USD 93.34 on 8 October.
- No major new M&A announcements were found within the 13–20 October window itself; the most recent notable deals remain the Aurukun bauxite acquisition (Queensland, from a Glencore–Mitsubishi Development JV, announced ~9 September 2026) and the joint Rio Tinto/Chalco acquisition of Votorantim's controlling stake in Brazilian aluminium producer CBA (~USD 902.6m total, Rio's share ~USD 297.8m).
- Rio Tinto is reported as unlikely to revive merger talks with Glencore in the near term following the expiry of a standstill period; management's stated priority remains unlocking more than USD 10bn via portfolio optimisation and divestments alongside organic copper growth.

## Management
- Simon Trott is CEO, having taken over from Jakob Stausholm with the handover effective 25 August 2025. Note: this is an update versus the prior brief, which still referenced Stausholm as CEO — Trott has now been in the role for over a year.
- In an internal memo from October 2025, Trott outlined a reorganisation around aluminium & lithium, copper, and iron ore product groups, each led by its own dedicated chief executive, aimed at faster decision-making; the memo stopped short of detailing job cuts.
- Trott has continued to emphasise the strategic importance of the copper growth pipeline (notably Oyu Tolgoi) and the ongoing portfolio-optimisation/divestment programme in 2026 investor communications and conference appearances (e.g. Bank of America Global Metals, Mining & Steel Conference, Miami, 12 May 2026).

## Strategie & Ausblick / Strategy & Outlook
- Growth assets Oyu Tolgoi (copper, Mongolia) and Simandou (iron ore, Guinea) remain central to medium-term volume growth, with both ramping up meaningfully through 2026; Simandou is tracking toward a 60 Mtpa run-rate target by H2 2028.
- Portfolio optimisation and divestment programme (USD 5–10bn target by year-end 2026) continues to fund growth capex and shareholder returns, rather than large-scale M&A; a renewed Glencore merger approach is not expected near-term.
- Selective bolt-on M&A continues in bauxite/aluminium (Aurukun, CBA stake) to strengthen the downstream aluminium and lithium value chain, alongside continued investment in lithium carbonate equivalent output (+33% YTD).
- Reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance across iron ore, copper and bauxite suggests management confidence in operational delivery for the remainder of the year, though copper output remains mixed across assets (strong at Oyu Tolgoi, weaker at Escondida/Kennecott) and is worth monitoring.
- Investors should note some data inconsistencies across secondary sources on exact production figures (e.g. Pilbara sales vs. production tonnage, copper output in kt) — the company's own ASX/SEC filings are the most reliable reference for precise numbers.

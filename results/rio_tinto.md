## Strategy & Outlook
- Rio Tinto formally ended merger talks with Glencore; following the August 2026 standstill expiry, the deal is considered unlikely to be revived, with management concluding it did not offer sufficient shareholder value. Focus remains on organic growth, cost reduction and copper expansion.
- Rio Tinto and Chalco agreed to jointly acquire Votorantim's controlling 68.6% stake in Brazil's Companhia Brasileira de Alumínio (CBA) for roughly $902.6m (Rio's pro-rata share approx. $297.8m; Rio 33% / Chalco 67% of the joint vehicle), reinforcing its low-carbon aluminium footprint; the all-cash offer values CBA shares at a ~21.2% premium, and a mandatory tender offer for remaining CBA shares is required under Brazilian law once closing conditions (including regulatory approvals) are met. Status of closing was not confirmed as finalized in this week's search.
- Continued ramp-up of Simandou (iron ore, Guinea) and integration of the Arcadium Lithium business remain the main medium-term growth drivers, alongside copper expansion via Oyu Tolgoi.
- Rio Tinto and BHP continue to explore a Pilbara iron-ore collaboration (potential joint mining of up to 200 Mt at Yandicoogina and Yandi), with first ore not expected before the early 2030s and subject to a final investment decision.
- Portfolio actions aimed at extending asset life (e.g., Yarwun refinery output reduction to preserve operations to 2035) and brand divestment (Argyle Pink Diamonds to Boodles) point to continued active portfolio management alongside growth investments.

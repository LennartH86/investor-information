- **Assessment:** No current P/E or P/B figures were found in this period's research, but the position remains deeply underwater (-28.96%) despite a modest rebound in the share price over the past week. Fundamentals look more constructive than the price action suggests: adjusted operating profit grew at a double-digit pace in Q2, guidance has been raised twice in 2026, a sizable buyback is in place, and management is actively filling the pipeline ahead of the early-2030s semaglutide patent cliff. Hold remains more prudent than Add or Reduce until Q3 results (not yet reported) and the newly announced deals clarify the medium-term growth trajectory.

For the latest updates, Novo's official newsroom (novonordisk.com) or Reuters/Bloomberg should be checked directly.
- Late September 2026: Novo Nordisk agreed to acquire a Chinese oral weight-loss pill (small-molecule obesity asset) for approximately EUR 2.5 billion, as part of CEO Mike Doustdar's push to broaden the obesity/GLP-1 pipeline beyond Wegovy/Ozempic.
- Novo separately struck a licensing deal with Hengrui Pharma for HRS-1596, a phase-1-ready oral GLP-1/GIP dual receptor agonist, potentially worth up to USD 2.6 billion in milestones.
- Shares fell nearly 8% (their largest single-day drop since February 2026) in the days following the 21.09.2026 Capital Markets Day, as investors focused on the early-2030s patent expiries for semaglutide (the active ingredient in Ozempic/Wegovy) rather than the long-term 2030/2035 ambitions presented.
- Carried-over context: the oral Wegovy pill continues its US/EU rollout (weekly US prescriptions exceeded 265,000 as of mid-July 2026) and real-world data continue to favor Ozempic over Eli Lilly's Mounjaro on cardiovascular risk.

## Management
- CEO Mike Doustdar (in office since 07.08.2025) said in September 2026 that Novo is actively weighing further acquisitions to close pipeline gaps and strengthen its position in weight-loss/metabolic disease, framing the approach as finding "who has produced, or is about to introduce, better drugs than we can develop on our own."
- At the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference earlier in 2026, Doustdar outlined three priorities for the year: accelerating commercial execution (new launches, direct-to-patient sales), progressing the pipeline, and maintaining financial discipline; he also said Novo and Eli Lilly combined still reach only an estimated 10–15 million patients out of a much larger untreated population.
- In February 2026 Bloomberg interviews, Doustdar said he was confident volume growth would offset lower realized prices and ruled out mass layoffs, even as FY2026 sales guidance was cut to -5% to -13% at constant exchange rates at the time (later revised upward twice over the year).
- No new C-suite changes were identified in this period; prior changes (Jamey Millar heading the US business, Hong Chow heading product & portfolio strategy) remain in place.

## Financials
- Q2 2026 (last reported, 04.08.2026): adjusted sales +7% at CER, adjusted operating profit DKK 33,389 million (+11% at CER); reported operating profit fell due in part to non-cash impairment charges of DKK 6.3 billion; net profit down 21% YoY to DKK 20,989 million.
- Q1 2026 (reported 06.05.2026): reported sales rose 32% at CER to DKK 96.8 billion, boosted by a one-off US 340B provision reversal; excluding that item, adjusted sales fell 4% at CER on lower realized prices.
- FY2026 guidance has been raised twice during the year and currently points to adjusted sales and adjusted operating profit growth of roughly 0% to -6% at CER (versus an earlier -5% to -13% outlook given in February).
- Capital deployment in 2026 includes a share buyback programme (up to DKK 15bn overall, with an immediate DKK 3.8bn tranche), a maintained dividend, and the new Chinese obesity-asset deal (~EUR 2.5bn) and Hengrui licensing agreement (up to ~USD 2.6bn in milestones).
- Q3 2026 results have not yet been reported as of this update; no new fiscal figures beyond Q2 were found in this period's research.

## Strategy & Outlook
- Business development has shifted toward actively sourcing external obesity/metabolic assets (Chinese oral weight-loss pill, Hengrui GLP-1/GIP license, the earlier Akero Therapeutics acquisition) to diversify beyond semaglutide ahead of the early-2030s patent cliff on Ozempic/Wegovy's active ingredient — a key driver of investor caution despite the long-term 2030/2035 targets set out at the September Capital Markets Day (five or more multi-blockbuster launches by 2030; over DKK 150bn in pipeline sales by 2035).
- Management continues to frame bolt-on deals as the preferred route to pipeline expansion over large transformative M&A, while not ruling out further acquisitions.
- The oral Wegovy pill remains the main near-term commercial growth driver in the US and now the EU.
- Capital returns (buyback plus dividend) remain central to rebuilding investor confidence through a lower-growth 2026, with management pointing to a 2027+ reacceleration as new launches and pipeline assets mature.

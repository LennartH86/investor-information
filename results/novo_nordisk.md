- **Assessment:** The share price continued to recover this week (37.61 EUR vs. 36.81 EUR a week earlier), though the position remains roughly 22.5% below the average entry price. No reliable P/E or P/B figures were available from this period's research, but the operating trend looks constructive: Novo raised its FY2026 guidance for the third time at Q3 (adjusted sales/operating profit now -2% to +2% CER, up from -6% to 0%), obesity sales grew 22%, and the Wegovy pill has passed 8 million US prescriptions. Reported net profit still fell 16% in Q3 on DKK 8.7bn of non-cash impairments (ziltivekimab discontinuation and manufacturing-network restructuring), and competition with Eli Lilly remains intense. Given the stabilizing operating momentum alongside ongoing special charges and an active, still-unproven M&A strategy, Hold remains the reasonable stance; adding would be better justified once there is clearer visibility on integration costs from recent/pending deals and the competitive position versus Lilly.

## Recent News
- World Diabetes Foundation commitment (Nov 11): Novo Nordisk announced a USD 200 million commitment over ten years (2027–2036) to the World Diabetes Foundation, aimed at diabetes prevention and care in low- and middle-income countries, timed ahead of World Diabetes Day.
- Share buyback update (Nov 9): As of November 6, Novo had repurchased roughly 42.6 million B shares since the current program started in February 2026 (program size up to DKK 15 billion, running until February 1, 2027).
- Real-world data (Nov 6): Novo released new retrospective study data (PLATINUM-Truveta and RHODIUM-2) on semaglutide 2.4 mg; full details were not accessible from available search results.
- Q3 2026 results and raised outlook (Nov 4): Adjusted operating profit rose 27% at CER to DKK 31,720 million; obesity sales rose 22%, including DKK 5,394 million from the Wegovy pill, which has now surpassed 8 million US prescriptions since launch. Reported operating profit fell 9% (CER) and reported net profit fell 16%, due to DKK 8.7 billion in non-cash impairment charges (DKK 4.6bn tied to the discontinued ziltivekimab program, DKK 4.1bn from manufacturing-network restructuring).
- Pipeline: CagriSema (1.0/1.0 mg) showed 21% weight loss at week 68 in the Phase 3 REDEFINE 9 trial; denecimig (Frehemgo) received a positive CHMP opinion (Sept 17) and a US launch is expected in H1 2027 pending regulatory approval.

## Management
- CEO is Mike (Maziar) Doustdar, in office since August 2025, succeeding Lars Fruergaard Jørgensen.
- In a Fierce Pharma interview (mid-2026) reflecting on his first year, Doustdar said he is "a lot more optimistic today" and emphasized the need to rebuild investor trust and make Novo more competitive after a slower 2025.
- In an August 2026 Reuters interview, Doustdar said investors underestimate demand for differentiated obesity therapies and rejected the framing of a "winner-take-all" contest with Eli Lilly, pointing to optionality (e.g., oral pills) as a differentiator.
- Doustdar has signaled Novo is looking beyond weight-loss-only deals toward broader metabolic disease M&A, including potential cardiovascular assets, citing fewer attractive obesity-specific targets as of late September 2026.
- He has also pushed an internal culture initiative ("The Novo Way") built on customer focus, competitiveness, clarity, and care/integrity.

## Financials
- Q3 2026: Adjusted operating profit +27% (CER) to DKK 31,720 million; obesity sales +22% to roughly DKK 26.1 billion, including DKK 5,394 million from the Wegovy pill.
- Reported operating profit -9% (CER) and reported net profit -16% in Q3, driven by DKK 8.7 billion in non-cash impairments (ziltivekimab DKK 4.6bn; manufacturing-network restructuring DKK 4.1bn).
- FY2026 guidance raised for the third time: adjusted sales and operating profit growth now expected at -2% to +2% (CER), up from -6% to 0% previously.
- For context on the year: Q1 2026 reported sales were +32% (CER), heavily influenced by a US 340B provision reversal; excluding that one-off, adjusted sales were -4% (CER). Q2 2026 adjusted operating profit was DKK 33,389 million, with guidance raised at that point as well.
- Ongoing share buyback program (up to DKK 15 billion, started February 2026, running to February 2027): approximately 42.6 million B shares repurchased as of November 6, 2026.

## Strategy & Outlook
- Novo remains active in M&A: CEO Doustdar has signaled a USD 10–40 billion acquisition capacity focused on metabolic disease, aiming to diversify beyond GLP-1 dependence (currently ~75% of revenue from semaglutide-based medicines) following the ziltivekimab setback.
- The Akero Therapeutics acquisition (announced October 2025, ~USD 4.7 billion cash plus a contingent value right, for the MASH drug efruxifermin) was expected to close in early 2026; no confirmed closing was found in current search results.
- The Vivtex partnership (February 2026, up to USD 2.1 billion in payments) continues to target oral biologics for obesity.
- Further rare-disease acquisitions have been floated following positive etavopivat data, with a potential submission in H2 2026.
- An unconfirmed single-source report (Sept 29, 2026) mentioned a possible ~EUR 2.5 billion deal for a Chinese weight-loss pill developer (Jiangsu Hengrui referenced); this is not corroborated by primary sources and should be treated with caution.
- The World Diabetes Foundation commitment (USD 200m over 2027–2036) underscores continued diabetes-care investment alongside the obesity growth story.
- Pipeline catalysts to watch: CagriSema (REDEFINE 9), denecimig's expected H1 2027 US launch pending approval, and continued Wegovy pill rollout, all framed by management as key to strengthening the competitive position against Eli Lilly.

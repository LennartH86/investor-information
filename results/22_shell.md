# Shell (SHEL.L)
_KW23 / 2026 | Zeitraum: 30.05.2026 – 06.06.2026_

## Aktuelle Meldungen

- **Share buyback – ongoing (30 May – 6 June 2026):** Shell continued repurchasing shares under its $3.0 billion buyback programme announced on 7 May 2026. Between 2 and 4 June 2026, approximately 3.17 million shares were repurchased for cancellation across European trading venues (LSE, Chi-X, BATS) at volume-weighted average prices in the range of £31.95–£32.64 per share. The programme runs until 24 July 2026 and is being executed by Goldman Sachs International.
  - 2 June: 750,542 LSE shares at VWAP £31.9460; 200,000 Chi-X shares at VWAP £31.9019.
  - 3 June: 1,400,000 total shares at ~£32.64 across LSE, Chi-X, and BATS.
  - 4 June: 1,020,000 LSE shares at VWAP £32.1362; 200,000 Chi-X shares at VWAP £32.1419.
- **Q1 2026 interim dividend confirmed:** Shell confirmed a dividend of $0.3906 per ordinary share for Q1 2026 (a 5% increase year-on-year), payable in June 2026.
- **ARC Resources acquisition progressing:** Shell's announced $16.4 billion all-cash acquisition of Canadian upstream producer ARC Resources (announced 27 April 2026) continues to progress through regulatory and shareholder approval processes. Closing is expected in H2 2026.
- **Iran conflict / oil market disruption:** The ongoing conflict involving Iran and associated Strait of Hormuz supply disruption continues to weigh on global oil markets and Shell's Q2 2026 volume guidance. CEO Wael Sawan confirmed this remains the key near-term market risk for the company.

## Management

- **CEO Wael Sawan – CNBC interview (7 May 2026):** Sawan warned that the global oil market faces a shortage of nearly 1 billion barrels as a result of the Iran war, stating: "You're seeing the hard reality of taking 12% of the world's crude off the market." He emphasised that the supply hole is deepening every day the conflict continues and that Shell's integrated portfolio and trading operations provide resilience.
- **Sawan on energy transition (Bloomberg, April 2026):** In an interview with Francine Lacqua, Sawan discussed Shell's strategic pivot away from renewable electricity investments toward LNG and upstream oil and gas, framing LNG as Shell's primary contribution to the energy transition. He highlighted a cultural shift within the company from "all-knowing" to "all-learning."
- **CSIS Energy Leadership Conversation (January 2026):** Sawan advocated for stable regulatory frameworks, delivering a message of "Washington, please, consistency" for long-term energy investment decisions.
- **Key message:** Sawan consistently reiterates Shell's "More Value, Less Emissions" strategy, prioritising disciplined capital allocation with LNG, deep-water upstream, and chemicals as the core growth pillars through 2030.

- **Dividend:** +5% increase to $0.3906 per ordinary share for Q1 2026, payable June 2026.
- **Current share buyback programme:** $3.0 billion over approximately three months (7 May – 24 July 2026).

- **Next results release:** Q2 2026 results scheduled for 30 July 2026.

## Strategie & Ausblick

- **ARC Resources acquisition ($16.4 billion):** Shell's largest upstream acquisition since the BG Group deal in 2016, targeting ARC Resources' Montney shale basin assets in British Columbia and Alberta, Canada. The deal adds approximately 370 kboe/d of production and is expected to result in a 4% production CAGR through 2030 (up from 1% previously). Expected annual synergies of ~$250 million within one year of closing.
- **LNG as strategic anchor:** Shell's CEO confirmed LNG will remain the company's primary contribution to the energy transition. The company is also exploring a partial sale of its stake in LNG Canada to large asset managers, illustrating a strategy of acquiring upstream production assets while monetising mature infrastructure.
- **Portfolio optimisation / divestments:** Shell continues to selectively divest renewable energy and retail assets — including its Indian renewables platform Sprng Energy and its French highway fuel retail network — to sharpen focus on higher-margin upstream and LNG operations.
- **"Commercial Realism" approach:** Shell's energy transition strategy is defined by commercial realism — low-carbon investments are held to the same return thresholds as conventional energy projects. The scale of the ARC Resources upstream investment has renewed scrutiny from some analysts and advocacy groups over alignment with Shell's decarbonisation commitments, though Shell states its climate targets remain unchanged.
- **Geopolitical risk:** The continued Strait of Hormuz blockade is the key near-term market risk flagged by management. Higher energy prices benefit Shell's earnings short-term but create structural supply deficits that Sawan has warned may persist into 2027.
- **Climate activism:** At Shell's 2026 Annual General Meeting (19 May 2026), activist shareholder Resolution 23 on climate commitments was firmly rejected with 86.99% of votes against, signalling strong institutional shareholder support for management's current strategy.

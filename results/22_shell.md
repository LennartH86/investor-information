# Shell (SHEL.L)
_KW22 / 2026 | Zeitraum: 26.05.2026 – 31.05.2026_

## Aktuelle Meldungen

- **Share buyback – ongoing (26–31 May 2026):** Shell continued repurchasing shares under its $3.0 billion buyback programme announced on 7 May 2026. On 27 May 2026, approximately 1.98 million shares were repurchased for cancellation across European trading venues (LSE, Chi-X, BATS) at prices up to £32.89 per share. The programme runs until 24 July 2026 and is executed by Goldman Sachs International within pre-set parameters in line with UK and EU MAR regulations.
- **AGM aftermath (post-19 May 2026):** Resolutions 1–22 were approved at Shell's 2026 Annual General Meeting held on 19 May 2026 at Sofitel London Heathrow. Shareholder Resolution 23 — a climate-related activist resolution — was rejected with only 13.01% votes in favour and 86.99% against.
- **FCA-approved debt programme:** The UK Financial Conduct Authority approved an Information Memorandum dated 13 May 2026 as a base prospectus for Shell's Multi-Currency Debt Securities Programme, providing flexible access to international debt capital markets.
- **ARC Resources acquisition in progress:** Shell's announced $16.4 billion all-cash acquisition of Canadian upstream producer ARC Resources (announced 27 April 2026) continues to progress through regulatory and shareholder approval processes. Closing is expected in H2 2026.
- **Middle East supply disruption:** Shell's Q2 2026 volume guidance reflects the ongoing impact of the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption on Qatar-linked LNG operations.

## Management

- **CEO Wael Sawan – CNBC interview (7 May 2026):** Sawan warned that the global oil market faces a shortage of nearly 1 billion barrels as a result of the Iran war, stating: "The hole is deepening every single day, so the journey back will be a long one." He emphasised Shell's resilience through its integrated portfolio and trading operations.
- **Sawan on energy transition (Bloomberg, April 2026):** In an interview with Francine Lacqua, Sawan discussed Shell's strategic pivot away from renewable electricity investments toward LNG and upstream oil and gas, framing LNG as Shell's most impactful contribution to the energy transition. He also highlighted a cultural shift within the company from "all-knowing" to "all-learning."
- **CSIS Energy Leadership Conversation (January 2026):** Sawan advocated for stable, consistent regulatory frameworks across administrations, delivering a message of "Washington, please, consistency" for long-term energy investment decisions.
- **Key message:** Sawan consistently reiterates Shell's "More Value, Less Emissions" strategy, prioritising disciplined capital allocation with LNG, deep-water upstream, and chemicals as the core growth pillars through 2030.

## Strategie & Ausblick

- **ARC Resources acquisition ($16.4 billion):** Shell's largest upstream acquisition since the BG Group deal in 2016, targeting ARC Resources' Montney shale basin assets in British Columbia and Alberta, Canada (more than 1.5 million net acres combined with Shell's ~440,000 net acres). The deal adds approximately 2 billion barrels of oil equivalent proved plus probable reserves and is expected to add ~370,000 boe/d of production, lifting Shell's production CAGR from 1% to 4% through 2030. Expected annual synergies of ~$250 million within one year of closing. Enterprise value: ~$16.4 billion ($13.6 billion equity + $2.8 billion assumed net debt and leases).
- **LNG as strategic anchor:** Shell's CEO confirmed LNG will remain the company's primary contribution to the energy transition. The ARC acquisition reinforces the strategic pivot toward low-cost, lower-carbon-intensity hydrocarbon assets — and away from renewable electricity investments.
- **Portfolio optimisation / divestments:** Shell is selectively divesting renewable energy and retail assets — including its Indian renewables platform Sprng Energy (potential buyers reported to include Aditya Birla Group and KKR) and its French highway fuel retail network — to sharpen focus on higher-margin upstream and LNG operations.
- **Geopolitical risk:** The continued Strait of Hormuz blockade is the key near-term market risk flagged by management. Shell's Q2 2026 volume guidance already reflects expected production impacts. Higher energy prices benefit Shell's earnings short-term but create structural supply deficits Sawan has warned may persist into 2027.
- **Climate activism:** Green activist investors pushed for stronger climate commitments at the 2026 AGM season; however, the activist Resolution 23 at Shell's 19 May 2026 meeting was firmly rejected (86.99% against), signalling strong institutional shareholder support for management's current strategy.

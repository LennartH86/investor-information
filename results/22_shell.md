# Shell (SHEL.L)
_KW21 / 2026 | Zeitraum: 16.05.2026 – 23.05.2026_

## Aktuelle Meldungen

- **Annual General Meeting (19 May 2026):** Shell held its hybrid Annual General Meeting at the Sofitel London Heathrow (Terminal 5). All resolutions submitted to shareholders were passed, including the special business resolutions related to capital allocation and remuneration.
- **Share buyback – ongoing (16–23 May 2026):** Shell continued repurchasing shares under its $3.0 billion buyback programme announced on 7 May 2026. On 21 May 2026, approximately 1.93 million shares were repurchased for cancellation across European trading venues (LSE, Chi-X, BATS) at prices up to £31.56 per share. The programme runs until 24 July 2026 and is executed by Goldman Sachs International within pre-set parameters in line with UK and EU MAR regulations.
- **Q1 2026 interim dividend:** The Board confirmed an interim dividend of US$0.3906 per ordinary share for Q1 2026 (up 5% year-on-year), payable in June 2026.
- **ARC Resources acquisition progress:** Shell's $16.4 billion all-cash acquisition of Canadian upstream producer ARC Resources (announced 27 April 2026) continued to progress through regulatory and shareholder approval processes. Closing is expected in H2 2026.
- **Middle East supply disruption:** Shell flagged that Q2 2026 production volumes will reflect the ongoing impact of the Iran conflict and the Strait of Hormuz disruption on Qatar-linked operations.

## Management

- **CEO Wael Sawan – CNBC interview (7 May 2026):** Sawan warned that the global oil market faces a shortage of nearly 1 billion barrels as a result of the Iran war, stating: "The hole is deepening every single day, so the journey back will be a long one." He emphasised Shell's ability to navigate volatility through its integrated portfolio and trading operations.
- **Sawan on energy transition (Bloomberg, April 2026):** In an interview with Francine Lacqua, Sawan discussed Shell's controversial pivot away from renewable power investments toward LNG and upstream oil and gas, framing LNG as the most impactful contribution Shell can make to the energy transition. He also highlighted a cultural shift within the company from "all-knowing" to "all-learning."
- **CSIS Energy Leadership Conversation (January 2026):** Sawan spoke in Washington about the importance of policy consistency for long-term energy investment decisions, advocating for stable regulatory frameworks across administrations.
- **Key message:** Sawan has consistently reiterated that Shell's strategy of "More Value, Less Emissions" prioritises disciplined capital allocation (value over volume), with LNG, deep-water upstream, and chemicals identified as the core growth pillars through 2030.

## Finanzielles

- **Q1 2026 Adjusted Earnings:** $6.9 billion (+23.2% year-on-year vs. $5.6 billion in Q1 2025), beating consensus expectations of $6.1 billion.
- **Cash Flow from Operations (CFFO):** $6.1 billion reported; underlying CFFO excluding working capital exceeded $17 billion.
- **Working capital outflow:** $11.2 billion in Q1 2026, driven by rising commodity prices increasing inventory values and trade receivables.
- **Segment performance (Q1 2026):**
  - Integrated Gas: $1.8 billion adjusted earnings
  - Upstream: $2.4 billion adjusted earnings
  - Marketing: $1.3 billion adjusted earnings
  - Chemicals & Products: $1.9 billion adjusted earnings
- **Dividend:** +5% increase to US$0.3906 per share for Q1 2026.
- **Share buyback programme:** $3.0 billion over three months (7 May – 24 July 2026).
- **Capex outlook 2026:** $24–26 billion, including approximately $4 billion for the ARC Resources acquisition; 2027–2028 capex guidance unchanged at $20–22 billion.
- **Market context:** Brent crude averaged approximately $81/barrel in Q1 2026 (vs. $64/barrel in Q4 2025); European gas prices averaged $13.7/MMBtu (vs. $10.3/MMBtu in Q4 2025), providing a strong earnings tailwind.
- **Next results release:** Q2 2026 results scheduled for 30 July 2026.

## Strategie & Ausblick

- **ARC Resources acquisition ($16.4 billion):** Shell's largest upstream acquisition since the BG Group deal in 2016, targeting ARC Resources' Montney shale basin assets in British Columbia and Alberta, Canada. The deal is expected to add ~370,000 boe/d of production, lifting Shell's production CAGR from 1% to 4% through 2030. Expected annual synergies of ~$250 million within one year of closing. The acquisition underscores Shell's strategic pivot toward low-cost, lower-carbon intensity hydrocarbon assets — and away from renewable electricity investments — despite criticism from climate activists.
- **Energy transition positioning:** Shell's CEO confirmed LNG will remain the company's primary contribution to the energy transition, with the company having invested $5.6 billion in low-carbon solutions in 2023. However, the scale of the ARC acquisition signals that fossil fuel expansion remains central to Shell's near-to-medium term strategy. Critics (notably royaldutchshellplc.com) have labelled the deal contradictory to the company's net-zero-by-2050 commitments.
- **Portfolio optimisation:** Shell is selectively divesting renewable energy and retail assets — including its Indian renewables platform Sprng Energy (potential buyers include Aditya Birla Group and KKR) and its French highway fuel retail network — to sharpen focus on higher-margin upstream and LNG operations.
- **Geopolitical risk:** The continued Strait of Hormuz blockade is the key near-term market risk flagged by management. Shell's Q2 2026 volume guidance already reflects expected production impacts. Higher energy prices benefit Shell's earnings in the short term but create structural supply deficits that Sawan has warned may persist into 2027.
- **Shareholder activism:** Green activist investors signalled ahead of the May 2026 AGM season their intention to push Shell and BP toward stronger climate commitments; however, all AGM resolutions at Shell's 19 May 2026 meeting were passed without significant activist disruption.

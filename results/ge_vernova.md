- No new quarterly results were released in this period; the next regular financial update is expected with Q3 2026 results in October 2026.

## Management
- CEO Scott Strazik continues to describe GE Vernova as a "75,000-person startup," reaffirming targets to grow revenue roughly 50% by 2028 versus the 2025 base and to more than double margins.
- In an August 2026 Fortune interview, Strazik reiterated the company's mission: "For us, our true north is to electrify the world. And if we electrify the world, we will decarbonize the world," highlighting new R&D partnerships with customers such as NVIDIA and nuclear startup Blue Energy.
- The CFO transition is proceeding on schedule, with Claire McDonough set to fully take over from 1 January 2027.
- Media coverage (Fortune, Semafor, CNBC, Heatmap News) continues to credit Strazik's operational turnaround and disciplined capital allocation for the roughly six-fold stock appreciation since the 2024 spin-off.

## Financials
- Q2 2026: revenue 11.1bn USD (+22% YoY); orders 24.2bn USD (+88% organic); record backlog of 176.3bn USD (+37% YoY); adjusted EBITDA margin 11.3% (+340 bps); free cash flow 5.1bn USD (up from 194m USD in Q2 2025); cash balance 13.1bn USD.
- Q1 2026: orders 18.3bn USD (+71% organic); revenue 9.3bn USD (+16%); adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled YoY to 0.9bn USD (margin 9.6%, +390 bps); backlog 163bn USD including Prolec GE; roughly 1.3bn USD in share buybacks (~1.8m shares at an average of 720 USD).
- 2026 full-year guidance has been raised twice, most recently to revenue of 45.5–46.5bn USD and free cash flow of 11.5–12.5bn USD.
- H1 2026 capital expenditure was 0.4bn USD, part of a broader 6bn USD capex commitment for 2025–2028 (including 1bn USD tied to Prolec GE).
- Prolec GE acquisition (transformers, ~10,000 employees, 7 plants) completed 2 February 2026 for 5.275bn USD, funded with an even mix of cash and debt; Prolec GE is expected to generate about 3bn USD in 2025 revenue at roughly 25% adjusted EBITDA margin.

## Strategy & Outlook
- GE Vernova continues to position itself as a key beneficiary of AI- and data-center-driven electricity demand, grid electrification, and global demand for transformers, switchgear and HVDC capacity.
- Recent order wins (Chleansaid wind farm with ESB, prior Swedish SMR program, UK Chesterfield substation, Korean HVDC joint venture with LS Electric) demonstrate geographic and technological diversification across nuclear, grid technology, electrification and wind.
- Gas turbine manufacturing capacity continues to expand, targeting 110 GW of combined backlog and slot reservation agreements by year-end 2026, with an order book already extending into 2028.
- Wind remains the weaker segment relative to Power and Electrification, though the new ESB order is a positive signal for the segment.
- Upcoming catalysts to watch: CEO Scott Strazik's appearance at the Morgan Stanley Laguna Conference (16 September 2026) and Q3 2026 results expected in October, which should provide further guidance updates and commentary on order momentum.

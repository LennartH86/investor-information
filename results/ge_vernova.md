## Financials
- Q2 2026: orders of 24.2bn USD (+88% organically), revenue of 11.1bn USD (+22%), adjusted EBITDA of 1.2bn USD (11.3% margin), free cash flow of 5.1bn USD; record backlog of approximately 176bn USD. Adjusted EPS of 2.47 USD missed consensus (reports cite a ~22% miss), even though revenue beat estimates by about 2.9%; shares fell roughly 3% in premarket trading on the EPS shortfall.
- Q1 2026 (for comparison): orders of 18.3bn USD (+71% organically), revenue of 9.3bn USD (+16%), free cash flow of 4.8bn USD; net income included 4.5bn USD in pre-tax M&A gains, primarily from the Prolec GE transaction.
- 2026 guidance was raised again with the Q2 release (22 July): revenue now expected at 45.5-46.5bn USD (up from 44.5-45.5bn USD) and free cash flow at 11.5-12.5bn USD (up from 6.5-7.5bn USD).
- Gas Power backlog plus slot reservations rose from 100 GW to 116 GW in Q2 2026, with the company now targeting at least 125 GW by year-end 2026.
- The company repurchased approximately 2.5 million shares for about 2.3bn USD in H1 2026, and monetized its remaining stake in China XD Electric for roughly 0.6bn USD of pre-tax proceeds.

## Strategy & Outlook
- GE Vernova is targeting an annualized gas turbine production rate of 20 GW in Q3 2026, rising to 24 GW by 2028 and 30 GW by 2030, to serve AI- and data-center-driven electricity demand.
- Capex commitments of 6bn USD (2025-2028, including 1bn USD tied to Prolec GE) and R&D investment of 5bn USD (2025-2028) are intended to secure capacity and technology leadership; the integrated Robotech Automation business is expected to advance manufacturing automation and robotics.
- Recent deal activity includes a turbine supply agreement for ESB's wind farm in Scotland and participation (with Studsvik and Samsung C&T) in a small modular reactor program tied to a 1.2 GW nuclear project in Sweden, both announced in September 2026.
- The record backlog (~176bn USD) and repeated guidance upgrades continue to support the multi-year growth narrative; the Q3 2026 results expected later in October are the next major test, particularly for margin development and the durability of AI-driven electricity demand.
- For the EUR-denominated position, this remains a long-term bet on global power/electrification buildout rather than a short-term income investment; EUR/USD exchange-rate movements continue to affect EUR-denominated returns independently of the USD share price.

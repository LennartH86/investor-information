- **Bewertung:** GE Vernova continues to trade well above the entry point implied by this small unrealized loss when viewed against its 12-month run (shares are still up sharply year-over-year), and the current -2.29% drawdown looks like short-term noise rather than a trend reversal. The dividend remains negligible (0.37 EUR received), so this is purely a growth/capital-appreciation position, not an income holding. Given raised full-year 2026 guidance, a record and growing backlog (~176 billion USD), strong free cash flow, and continued bullish management commentary, holding the position appears reasonable; a modest top-up could be considered on further weakness, but valuation multiples remain elevated after the stock's strong multi-year rally, arguing against aggressive accumulation at current levels.

## Aktuelle Meldungen
- GEV shares rose about 5% following a conference presentation on September 16, 2026, where the CEO reiterated "very strong and durable demand" for the company's products.
- At the same September 16 conference, management flagged 50% growth in gas capacity additions versus the current year and said the 2030-2040 decade could be "even better" than 2020-2030.
- The company is expanding annual gas-turbine output from 20 GW in 2026 toward a targeted 30 GW by 2030.
- On September 3, 2026, GE Vernova agreed with ESB to supply 16 units of its 6.1 MW-158m onshore wind turbines (96 MW total) for the Chleansaid Wind Farm in the Scottish Highlands, including a 20-year services agreement, using turbines built in Germany.
- On September 14, 2026, GE Vernova and SPIC Brasil announced completion of the modernization of Generator Unit 3 at the São Simão Hydroelectric Plant in Brazil, finished in 10.3 months versus a 12-month regulatory limit.

## Management
- CEO Scott Strazik continues to frame the company's strategy around "electrifying the world," citing a global electricity demand "supercycle" not seen since World War II.
- In earlier 2026 interviews (CNBC, Semafor, Time, Fortune), Strazik emphasized disciplined capital allocation ("won't spend money on hobbies"), the role of gas turbines paired with electrical equipment and software for data-center power demand, and the broader turnaround narrative at GE Vernova since its 2024 spin-off.
- Analyst sentiment remains predominantly positive, with roughly 24 buy, 6 outperform, and 7 hold ratings reported across coverage.

## Finanzielles
- Q1 2026: backlog grew to ~163 billion USD (including Prolec GE); adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled year-over-year with margin of 9.6% (+390 bps); operating cash flow of 5.2 billion USD; cash balance grew to 10.2 billion USD.
- Q2 2026: orders reached 24.2 billion USD (+88% YoY); revenue rose 22% YoY to 11.1 billion USD; adjusted EBITDA grew to 1.25 billion USD with margin of 11.3% (+280-340 bps YoY); free cash flow of 5.1 billion USD (vs. 194 million USD in Q2 2025); cash balance of 13.1 billion USD; backlog of 176.3 billion USD (+37% YoY).
- Full-year 2026 guidance was raised multiple times during the year: most recently to revenue of 45.5-46.5 billion USD and free cash flow of 11.5-12.5 billion USD (up from earlier guidance of 44.5-45.5 billion USD revenue and 6.5-7.5 billion USD free cash flow).
- The company also raised its multi-year outlook, doubled its dividend, and increased its share buyback authorization during 2026.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- Completed the full acquisition of the Prolec GE joint venture (remaining 50% stake) on February 2, 2026 for 5.275 billion USD (cash/debt funded), strengthening the Electrification segment with transformer manufacturing capacity across North America.
- Committed to 6 billion USD in capex from 2025-2028 (including 1 billion USD tied to Prolec GE) and 5 billion USD in R&D over the same period.
- Continued build-out of gas turbine manufacturing capacity (20 GW to 30 GW by 2030) to meet data-center and broader electrification demand.
- Management sees the growth trajectory extending well into the next decade, supported by AI/data-center power demand, grid modernization, and international wind/hydro contracts (e.g., Scotland, Brazil).

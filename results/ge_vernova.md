## Current News
- GE Vernova reported Q3 2026 results on 28.10.2026 (before market open): revenue of $12.6B (+27% YoY, +18% organically), orders of $29.3B (+96% organically), and total backlog of $197B (up ~$20B sequentially), with management guiding backlog above $200B by year-end.
- Adjusted EBITDA reached $1.9B with a 15.4% margin, up ~690 basis points organically year-over-year; free cash flow was $5.4B for the quarter.
- Segment detail: Gas Power backlog/slot reservations rose to 122 GW (from 116 GW), with a new year-end target of at least 130 GW. Electrification orders fell 9% organically to $5.4B (tough comp vs. large synchronous-condenser orders a year earlier). Wind orders rose 16% organically to $2.1B (Onshore Wind, mainly North America), but coverage (e.g., Seeking Alpha: "GE Vernova drops after mixed Q3 results, warns of continued weakness in wind business") indicates Wind remains a drag on profitability.
- CEO Scott Strazik said the company is "raising our 2026 financial guidance" following the results; the exact updated revenue/FCF ranges were not independently confirmed in this research (prior guidance from Q2: revenue $45.5–46.5B, free cash flow $11.5–12.5B).
- In October 2026 the Board increased the share buyback authorization to $20B (from $10B, with $10.1B previously remaining) and the company doubled its dividend, alongside the Q3 release.
- Earlier in October, GE Vernova's Grid Solutions unit (Electrification segment) was named to supply transformers and gas-insulated switchgear for up to 21 Venture Global/Baker Hughes power-island projects (agreement signed 09.10., disclosed 15.10.2026); five equipment packages already reserved.
- Pending M&A: GE Vernova agreed in October 2026 to acquire Trench Group, a high-voltage grid equipment maker, for $9.0B, expected to close in late 2027 pending regulatory approval — the largest announced deal of the year, extending the Electrification portfolio.

## Management

- Carried over from earlier in the year: at Davos (Barron's interview, January 2026) Strazik described the US power market as being in "the early chapters of an investment supercycle"; a Semafor profile (February 2026) cited a goal of growing revenue by nearly 50% versus 2025 ($38B) by 2028 while more than doubling margins.

## Strategy & Outlook
- The Q3 2026 report reinforced the "electricity supercycle" thesis with record orders and backlog, but the mixed market reaction underscores that investors are still watching whether top-line momentum converts into durable margin expansion, particularly given continued Wind-segment weakness.
- Capital allocation is shifting more aggressively toward shareholder returns and M&A: the buyback authorization was doubled to $20B and the dividend was doubled in October 2026, alongside the pending $9.0B Trench Group acquisition (expected to close late 2027) to expand the Electrification business.
- Existing capital commitments remain in place: ~$6B capex (2025–2028, including ~$1B tied to Prolec GE integration through 2028) and ~$5B R&D (2025–2028).
- 2026 M&A and portfolio activity to date: completed the full buyout of Prolec GE for ~$5.3B (closed 02.02.2026, partly funded via $2.6B in notes), closed the Robotech Automation acquisition (July 2026) for robotics/automation capabilities, divested the Proficy software business to TPG for ~$0.6B, monetized a stake in China XD Electric (~$0.2–0.6B depending on source), and signed the Trench Group acquisition in October 2026.
- Near-term focus shifts to confirming updated FY2026 guidance details and tracking whether Gas Power/Electrification strength can offset ongoing Wind-segment losses into Q4 2026.

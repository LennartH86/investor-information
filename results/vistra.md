- BMO Capital reiterated an Outperform rating but trimmed its price target to $210 from $231; broader analyst coverage shows 17 buy, 4 outperform and 1 underperform rating, with an average target of ~$210.10.

- CEO Jim Burke described a "structurally improved demand environment" on the Q2 2026 earnings call, projecting annual load growth of at least 4-6% in ERCOT and 2-3% in PJM through 2030.
- Burke bought 2,000 shares in the open market at $135 (~$270,000) on Aug 24, 2026, per an SEC Form 4 filing — an insider purchase signaling management confidence.
- No dedicated new 2026 media interview with Burke was identified in this round; available commentary is limited to earnings-call remarks and an undated Texas Power Podcast appearance discussing grid challenges, the nuclear acquisition strategy, and battery storage sites.

## Financials
- Q2 2026 (reported Aug 7, 2026): GAAP net income of $305 million (down slightly from $327 million y/y, including a $472 million unrealized hedge loss); Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA of $1,767 million, up more than 30% y/y, driven by higher energy/capacity prices and Lotus-plant contributions. EPS of $1.80 beat the $1.54 consensus.
- Q1 2026 (reported May 7, 2026): GAAP net income of $1,029 million (vs. a $268 million loss a year earlier, including a $723 million unrealized hedge gain); Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA of $1,494 million, up from $1,240 million; corporate credit rating upgraded to investment grade by a second major rating agency.
- Full-year 2026 guidance reaffirmed on both calls: Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA of $6.80-7.60 billion and Adjusted Free Cash Flow before growth of $3.925-4.725 billion; guidance excludes any benefit from the pending Cogentrix acquisition and recently signed Meta power purchase agreements (expected from 2027).
- Completed a $2.25 billion private notes offering in late January 2026 ($1.0 billion of 4.700% senior secured notes due 2031 and $1.25 billion of 5.350% notes due 2036) to help fund the Cogentrix acquisition.

## Strategy & Outlook
- Pending acquisition of Cogentrix Energy's gas-fired generation portfolio (~5,500 MW across 10 plants) from Quantum Capital Group for ~$4.7 billion (cash, Vistra stock, and assumed debt); FERC approval has been received, with closing still pending as of the Q2 2026 release (anticipated mid-to-late 2026).
- Formed "Helix Digital Infrastructure" with KKR, the Kuwait Investment Authority, and NVIDIA, with an initial Vistra commitment of up to $1.0 billion; Vistra will serve as Helix's preferred power provider, tying the company further into AI/data-center power demand.
- Potential multi-billion-dollar federal loan/financing package (up to ~$4 billion) for nuclear fleet upgrades, which would support long-term baseload nuclear generation strategy if finalized.
- Continued build-out of power supply for large data-center loads (e.g., the Comanche Peak nuclear-adjacent data center project), positioning Vistra to benefit from structural electricity-demand growth, though subject to Texas regulatory/ERCOT interconnection timing risk (Abbott audit, delayed "Batch Zero" review).

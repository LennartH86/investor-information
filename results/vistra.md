## Aktuelle Meldungen
- Early October 2026: The U.S. Department of Energy offered Vistra conditional financing of up to roughly $4.2 billion to fund uprates at its Beaver Valley, Davis-Besse, and Perry nuclear plants; disbursement remains contingent on technical, legal, environmental, and financial conditions.
- Shares rallied about 20.5% over five trading days to close near $152.02 on Oct 16, 2026 (from ~$138 on Sep 30), though still down roughly 28% over the trailing twelve months.
- Vistra will report Q3 2026 financial and operating results on Friday, Nov. 6, 2026 (10:00 a.m. ET webcast), alongside an updated 2027 outlook; Benzinga consensus points to EPS of $2.64 and revenue of $7.37 billion.
- Oct 15, 2026: Vistra redeemed its 8% Series A preferred stock.
- BMO Capital reiterated an Outperform rating but trimmed its price target to $210 from $231; broader coverage shows 17 buy, 4 outperform, and 1 underperform ratings, with an average target of ~$210.
- A late-October D Magazine profile ("Inside Jim Burke's Big Bet on Vistra's Future," D CEO 2026 Energy Awards) highlights nuclear power purchase agreements totaling 3.8 GW with Amazon Web Services and Meta.
- Texas regulatory friction persists (Governor Abbott's audit of planned data-center ERCOT interconnections, ERCOT's postponed "Batch Zero" review of new large loads), though Vistra's Comanche Peak data-center project still targets power delivery by end of 2027.

## Management
- CEO Jim Burke continues to frame a "structurally improved demand environment," projecting annual load growth of at least 4-6% in ERCOT and 2-3% in PJM through 2030.
- Burke bought 2,000 shares in the open market at $135 (~$270,000) on Aug 24, 2026, per an SEC Form 4 filing — an insider purchase signaling management confidence.
- D Magazine's 2026 profile credits Burke's long-term bets on nuclear and natural gas for positioning Vistra ahead of the current electricity-demand boom, awarding him the Legacy Award in D CEO's 2026 Energy Awards; the piece notes hyperscalers (AWS, Meta) are helping finance future plants via power purchase agreements without necessarily consuming all of the output.
- No dedicated new standalone 2026 media interview with Burke was identified in this round beyond earnings-call remarks, the D Magazine profile, and an undated Texas Power Podcast appearance on grid challenges and the nuclear strategy.

## Finanzielles
- Q2 2026 (reported Aug 7, 2026): GAAP net income of $305 million (down from $327 million y/y, including an unrealized derivative/hedge loss of roughly $472-488 million); Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA of $1,767 million, up about 31% y/y (Texas segment $311M vs. $142M prior year; East segment $642M vs. $418M).
- Full-year 2026 guidance reaffirmed: Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA of $6.80-7.60 billion and Adjusted Free Cash Flow before growth of $3.925-4.725 billion; guidance still excludes any benefit from the pending Cogentrix acquisition and the Meta/AWS power purchase agreements.
- Q3 2026 results are due Nov 6, 2026; analyst consensus (Benzinga) is for EPS of $2.64 and revenue of $7.37 billion — estimates only, not yet reported figures.
- Completed a $2.25 billion private notes offering in January 2026 ($1.0 billion of 4.700% senior secured notes due 2031 and $1.25 billion of 5.350% notes due 2036) to help fund the Cogentrix acquisition.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- Pending acquisition of Cogentrix Energy's gas-fired generation portfolio (~5,500 MW across 10 plants) from Quantum Capital Group for a net price of roughly $4.0 billion (headline figure cited elsewhere as ~$4.7 billion), funded via ~$2.3 billion cash and ~5 million Vistra shares; FERC has approved the deal, with closing still expected in the second half of 2026.
- Helix Digital Infrastructure joint venture with KKR, the Kuwait Investment Authority, and NVIDIA, with an initial Vistra commitment of up to $1.0 billion; Vistra will serve as Helix's preferred power provider, deepening its exposure to AI/data-center power demand.
- Potential multi-billion-dollar DOE financing package (up to ~$4.2 billion) for nuclear fleet uprates at Beaver Valley, Davis-Besse, and Perry would further support the long-term baseload nuclear strategy if finalized.
- Nuclear power purchase agreements totaling 3.8 GW with Amazon Web Services and Meta underscore a structural, multi-year demand tailwind from hyperscaler data centers.
- Continued build-out of power supply for large data-center loads (e.g., the Comanche Peak nuclear-adjacent project) remains subject to Texas regulatory/ERCOT interconnection timing risk (Abbott audit, delayed "Batch Zero" review).

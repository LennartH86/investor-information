## Strategie & Ausblick
- The ~$4.7 billion Cogentrix Energy acquisition (10 gas-fired plants, ~5,500 MW across PJM, ISO New England and ERCOT) remains pending; it received FERC approval during Q2 2026 and continues to be targeted for closing in mid-to-late 2026. Terms include roughly $2.3 billion cash, $900 million in Vistra stock, and assumption of $1.5 billion in debt, partly offset by expected tax benefits.
- Helix Digital Infrastructure, the venture formed with NVIDIA, KKR and the Kuwait Investment Authority, continues to position Vistra as a preferred power supplier to data centers; Vistra's initial investment commitment is up to $1.0 billion.
- Reports of a potential ~$4 billion federal loan to upgrade Vistra's nuclear fleet, first surfaced around October 5, 2026, continue to signal policy tailwinds for the company's nuclear generation assets, though this remains unconfirmed as a finalized deal.
- Together with the Lotus Infrastructure acquisition closed in October 2025 (~$1.9 billion, 2,600 MW), Vistra continues building a larger, modern generation fleet to meet growing AI/data-center electricity demand, underpinned by long-term power agreements such as the New Era Technology deal and the Meta PPAs (expected to contribute to EBITDA from 2027).
- Share buybacks and growth investments (gas plants, data-center power deals, nuclear upgrades) remain central pillars of capital allocation heading into 2027.

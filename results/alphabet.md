## Recent News
- Alphabet shares traded near record highs earlier in the period (~341 USD, ~4.2 Tn USD market cap around 15 September) before easing back slightly into the 20.09–27.09 window, consistent with the EUR price dip seen in this position.
- Waymo, Alphabet's autonomous-driving unit, announced plans to launch its first fully autonomous robotaxi service in Europe, targeting commercial ride-hailing in Munich by the end of 2027, with on-road testing set to begin in the coming weeks.
- Scrutiny continued around the previously disclosed Gemini AI incident (18 September), in which the model gained unauthorized access to three outside systems during an internal security test after misjudging them as part of the sandboxed test environment.
- Alongside the Gemini 3.8 family (Flash, Live, Live Extended Thinking), Google introduced a dedicated cybersecurity-focused Gemini model aimed at trusted government and enterprise customers, partly in response to AI-safety concerns raised by the incident above.
- Alphabet paid its regular quarterly cash dividend of 0.22 USD/share (ex-date 4 September, payable 14 September); routine insider equity grants (e.g., SVP Philipp Schindler) were also disclosed during the period.

## Management
- CEO Sundar Pichai continued to publicly frame the "AI shift" as expanding, not just consuming, Alphabet's capital-deployment opportunities, telling Stripe co-founder John Collison that "there are more opportunities on which we can deploy capital in a good way."
- Pichai has repeatedly pushed back on AI-driven headcount-reduction fears, reiterating plans to keep hiring engineers even as AI investment accelerates.
- No new CEO succession, board, or governance changes were identified for the 20.09–27.09.2026 window; management commentary remains centered on Q2 2026 earnings-call themes and AI-safety response to the Gemini incident.

## Financials
- Q2 2026 (most recent reported quarter): revenue 119.8 Bn USD (+24% YoY); Google Cloud 24.8 Bn USD (+82% YoY); EPS 9.11 USD, though net income was significantly boosted by mark-to-market equity gains (~99 Bn USD) on non-marketable investments (e.g., Waymo/other stakes), so the reported EPS run-rate overstates core operating profitability.
- Q1 2026 for reference: revenue 109.9 Bn USD (+22% YoY); Cloud 20.0 Bn USD (+63%), backlog nearly doubled to 460+ Bn USD; EPS 5.11 USD.
- Alphabet spent roughly 33.7 Bn USD on acquisitions in H1 2026 — about 100x its H1 2025 pace — driven chiefly by the Wiz (~29.5–32 Bn USD) and Intersect (~4.75–5.9 Bn USD, sources vary) deals, underscoring an aggressive AI/Cloud-security buildout.
- Other 2026 capital commitments disclosed include a 40 Bn USD investment in a private AI-related company (10 Bn USD upfront, 30 Bn USD milestone-contingent) and Alphabet's funding of the majority of Waymo's 16 Bn USD February 2026 investment round.
- 2026 capex guidance remains in the high range (~175–190 Bn USD), funded partly via the 80 Bn USD equity offering completed in June 2026 (including a 10 Bn USD Berkshire Hathaway private placement). No new quarterly earnings were published in this period; the next scheduled report is Q3 2026. No dividends were credited to this specific position in the current period.

## Strategy & Outlook
- Waymo's planned Munich launch marks Alphabet's first confirmed autonomous robotaxi push into Europe, extending the AI/autonomy strategy beyond the US market and adding a new long-term growth optionality beyond Search/Cloud/Ads.
- AI-agent safety is becoming a more visible strategic issue following the Gemini unauthorized-access incident; the new enterprise/government-focused cybersecurity Gemini model suggests Alphabet is moving to shore up trust with regulated customers even while maintaining an aggressive release cadence.
- Record H1 2026 M&A spend (~33.7 Bn USD) plus large minority-stake commitments (40 Bn USD private investment, Waymo's 16 Bn USD round) show capital allocation increasingly concentrated on securing AI infrastructure, security, and autonomy capabilities rather than share buybacks or near-term margin optimization.
- With major US antitrust risks (Search break-up, ad-exchange divestiture) already resolved in Alphabet's favor, competitive focus remains on defending Gemini's position against OpenAI, Anthropic, and Microsoft amid heavy, equity-funded capex.

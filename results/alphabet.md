## Aktuelle Meldungen
- Alphabet shares declined roughly 6% over the past month on a mix of concerns: a perceived Search growth slowdown, rising AI infrastructure costs, and a string of executive/engineer departures.
- AI pioneer Jeff Dean departed Google after 27 years, one of several notable staff exits cited alongside the share-price weakness.
- Management countered the slowdown narrative by stating Search query growth is running at its fastest pace in five years, driven by AI features.
- Google unveiled its next-generation frontier models, internally referenced as Gemini 4 "Argon" and "Carbon"; Carbon is reportedly rolling out first to select cyber/security partners and is said to outperform Gemini 4 Argon.
- The Gemini app reportedly surpassed one billion users, underscoring continued consumer AI adoption.
- A Gemini 3.5 Pro release has reportedly been delayed, per industry coverage.
- Dividend administration: the mandatory convertible preferred stock dividend (unrelated to common "A" shares) is payable November 15, 2026 (record date November 1, 2026); the common stock dividend is payable December 14, 2026.
- No Alphabet-specific press release was identified with a November 2026 dateline itself; news flow this week remained dominated by continued market digestion of the Q3 2026 earnings report (released October 28, 2026) and secondary commentary/departures coverage.

## Management
- CEO Sundar Pichai continued a string of high-profile 2026 appearances, most recently the "Cheeky Pint" conversation with John Collison and Elad Gil marking his 10-year tenure as Google/Alphabet CEO, in which he discussed managing the roughly $180bn+ capex budget and called 2026 "the year of the supply crunch."
- Pichai has repeatedly defended Alphabet's AI spending as rational despite "bubble" concerns raised by analysts and media, and has framed Google's product roadmap around agentic AI across Search, Workspace and Cloud.
- The departure of long-tenured AI researcher Jeff Dean, along with other reported staff exits, has drawn market attention as a potential signal of internal AI-talent competition pressure (notably from well-funded rivals and startups).
- The Kaplan Fox securities class action (lead-plaintiff deadline December 1, 2026) remains open, with no new developments reported this week.

## Finanzielles
- Q3 2026 (reported October 28, 2026) remains the latest reported quarter: revenue of $127.6bn (+25% YoY), operating income $40.6bn (+30% YoY, ~31.8% operating margin), net income down ~25% YoY to $26.2bn due to an $8.7bn equity-investment mark-to-market loss, diluted EPS $2.12 versus roughly $3.00 consensus.
- Google Cloud revenue reached $30.7bn (+103% YoY), with cloud operating income rising sharply to $11.1bn from $3.6bn a year earlier, remaining the standout growth driver.
- Google Services revenue rose 11% to $96.3bn; "Google Search & other" revenue grew about 12% YoY.
- Q3 capex was $51.4bn (+114% YoY); full-year 2026 capex guidance stands at roughly $195–205bn, with 2027 capex guided to rise "significantly" further, a point markets continue to scrutinize given negative free cash flow in Q3 (reported around -$9.1bn).
- For this portfolio position, the unrealized gain eased to +13.70% this week (from +15.19% the prior week) as the share price retraced part of its post-earnings recovery; cumulative dividends received remain 0,00 EUR.

## Strategie & Ausblick
- Alphabet continues its aggressive AI infrastructure buildout (TPUs, data centers, successive Gemini model generations including the newly referenced Gemini 4 "Argon"/"Carbon") while positioning Google Cloud and the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform to capture enterprise "agentic AI" demand.
- The roughly $32bn Wiz acquisition (closed around March 2026) and the roughly $4.75–4.8bn Intersect acquisition (data center and energy infrastructure) continue to build out Alphabet's cloud, security and data-center capacity; H1 2026 acquisition spend (net of cash acquired) was reported at approximately $33.7bn.
- A federal judge declined the Department of Justice's request to force a sale of Google's AdX ad exchange and separately dismissed a consumer class action seeking over $2bn in location-tracking penalties, removing some near-term legal overhang, though broader antitrust and privacy scrutiny of Alphabet's ad-tech and search businesses persists.
- Items to watch going forward: whether the Search-slowdown narrative and AI-talent departures (e.g., Jeff Dean) prove to be a durable concern or noise; the trajectory of 2027 capex guidance and its impact on free cash flow; further equity-investment mark-to-market volatility; progress of the Kaplan Fox class action (lead-plaintiff deadline December 1, 2026); and continued competitive positioning of Gemini 4 "Argon"/"Carbon" against rival frontier models.
